# The disc

What the disc holds for writing a level back onto it, reading by reading, with what each rests on: the image and where its room is, how a sector is built so that a console takes it as pressed, and the packs with the bytes each has to spare. Every figure here is measured on the NTSC-U image under `$KULA_DISC`: by `tools/kula_sector.py`, whose `--check` rebuilds the disc's own sectors and compares, by `tools/kula_place.py`, which puts a level into a pack on a copy of the image, and by `tools/kula_patch.py`, which writes the result out as a patch; the unit tests hold the readings to the disc wherever it is present. [level-format.md](level-format.md) is the level inside a pack, and [editor.md](editor.md) how a level is written from its fields.

## The image

The image is 257,795,664 bytes, which is 109,607 sectors of 2352 bytes and nothing over, one Mode 2 data track, so the cue sheet is the three lines `tools/kula_warp.py` writes beside a copy. Every sector of the 109,607 opens with the twelve-byte sync, carries its own address in the header as minutes, seconds and frames in BCD counted from the two-second pregap, so sector 1002 reads `00 15 27 02` and the last, 109606, `24 23 31 02`, and has 2 for its mode; both copies of the subheader agree on every sector. The primary volume descriptor is sector 16: type 1, `CD001`, system and application identifier `PLAYSTATION`, a volume identifier of 32 spaces, a volume space of 109,607 blocks of 2048 bytes, and no Joliet descriptor after it, so every file has exactly one directory record. Sector 17 is the terminator, 18 to 21 the two path tables with their copies, 22 the root directory and 23 `SYSTEM.CNF`, which boots `cdrom:SLUS_007.24;1`, the executable at 24 to 321. Sectors 0 to 15 are the system area: 0 to 3 empty, 4 to 11 the licence data, and 12 to 15 Form 2 sectors with nothing in them.

42 files lie in twelve directories of one sector each: the root at 22, `/XA` at 3946, and the ten worlds, HIRO at 322, ARCTIC 1012, ATLANT 1340, COWBOY 1675, FIELD 1991, HAZE 2321, HELL 2645, HILLS 2971, INCA 3293 and MARS 3616. The data files lie in address order with no gap between them but the directory sectors: each world's directory stands between the last pack of the world before it and its own artwork, so `COPYCAT.PAK` ends at 1011, ARCTIC's directory is 1012 and `ARCTIC.TGI` begins at 1013. A pack is therefore followed at once by the next file or by a directory, and cannot grow past the sectors it has. A directory record holds a file's extent and size in both byte orders, little-endian at 2 and 10 and big-endian at 6 and 14, and the two agree on every record of the disc; a pack's record lies in its world's directory sector, the world pack's at offset 96 and the finals pack's at 212 or 216, but for HIRO, whose directory has `COPYCAT.PAK` at 96, `HIRO.PAK` at 214 and `HIROFI.PAK` at 388.

The one room on the disc is `/3.00`, the last file: 31,752,000 bytes at sector 93953, 15,504 sectors whose user data is zero on every one, and a name the executable never spells. After it, sectors 109457 to 109606 are the track's postgap, 150 sectors with a sync and a header and zero from the subheader on, which is also what the EDC and ECC of nothing come to, so they check as they stand.

Of the 109,607 sectors, 31,601 are Form 1 and 78,006 Form 2. The Form 1 sectors are every data file, `/3.00`, the system area, the postgap and the picture frames of the two video streams; the Form 2 sectors are the four music files' XA audio and the sound of the streams. A Form 2 sector carries 2324 bytes of data, no ECC, and an EDC at 2348 over bytes 16 to 2347, which is the same CRC as below over a longer span and checks on all 78,006. Nothing here writes one.

## The sector

A Mode 2 Form 1 sector, as the disc has every one of its data sectors:

| bytes | holds |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 11 | sync: `00`, ten `FF`, `00` |
| 12 to 15 | minutes, seconds and frames of the address plus 150, in BCD, then the mode, 2 |
| 16 to 19 | the subheader: file, channel, submode, coding |
| 20 to 23 | the subheader again |
| 24 to 2071 | 2048 bytes of user data |
| 2072 to 2075 | the EDC, little-endian |
| 2076 to 2247 | 172 bytes of P parity |
| 2248 to 2351 | 104 bytes of Q parity |

The file and channel are 0 and the coding 0 on every Form 1 sector of the disc; the XA files use file 1 and channels 0 to 3, with coding 1 on the audio. The submode is `0x08`, data, on an ordinary sector of a file and `0x89`, data with end of record and end of file, on the last sector of every file outside `/XA`, of every directory, and of the terminator, the path tables and the root, 53 in all; the volume descriptor alone has `0x09`. The streams' picture frames carry `0x48`, real-time data, and `FINAL.STR` ends on `0xC8`; `0x00` is on 5,696 sectors, the postgap's 150 and the rest inside three of the music files and the logo stream, the empty sectors of their interleave. Among the Form 2 sectors `0x64` is the audio, `0xE4` the last sector of each of the music's channels and of the logo's sound, 17 in all, and `0x20` the four empty sectors of the system area. **A pack runs on `0x08` and ends on `0x89`**, on all 21, 85 sectors in all, so a pack of one sector is one `0x89` sector; the unit tests hold this to the disc.

**The EDC** is a CRC-32 over bytes 16 to 2071, the subheader and the user data, with the polynomial (x^16 + x^15 + x^2 + 1)(x^16 + x^2 + x + 1), which is x^32 + x^31 + x^16 + x^15 + x^4 + x^3 + x + 1, run least significant bit first from a zero register with no final inversion, which is a table built from the bit-reversed constant `0xD8018001`, and stored little-endian at 2072.

**The ECC** is a Reed-Solomon product code in GF(2^8) over x^8 + x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + 1, `0x11D`, laid out as ECMA-130 lays it out, over the 2064 bytes 12 to 2075, header, subheader, user data and EDC, with the four header bytes read as zero. That is the Mode 2 convention: the parity does not depend on the sector's address, so the same bytes carry the same EDC and ECC wherever they lie, and a file can be moved sector by sector with nothing but its addresses rewritten. The 2064 bytes are read as two planes of 1032, the even bytes and the odd, and each plane is coded alike. For P, a plane is 24 rows of 43 columns, byte n at row n // 43 and column n % 43, and each column is a codeword of 26 bytes, its 24 data bytes then two of parity; with m the column's offset in its first row, 2 x column + plane, the column's bytes are 12 + m, 12 + m + 86 and so on, 86 apart, and its parity goes to 2076 + m and 2076 + 86 + m. For Q, a plane with its 86 P bytes is 1118 bytes read as 26 rows of 43, and each of the 26 diagonals is a codeword of 45: the k-th of its 43 data bytes is the plane's byte (43 d + 44 k) mod 1118, in sector bytes 12 + (86 d + 88 k) mod 2236 + plane, and its parity goes to 2248 + m and 2248 + 52 + m with m = 2 d + plane. Both codes have the check matrix of a row of ones over a row of descending powers of alpha: a codeword V of n bytes has sum V_i = 0 and sum V_i alpha^(n - 1 - i) = 0, so with S the sum of the data bytes and T the sum of the data bytes weighted by alpha^(n - 1 - i), the first parity byte is (T + S) / (alpha + 1) and the second is the first plus S.

What settles the reading is the disc: rebuilt from its address, subheader and user data alone by `tools/kula_sector.py --sweep`, every one of the 31,601 Form 1 sectors of the image comes out bit for bit as the disc has it, in 51 seconds, and one flipped user byte fails both the EDC and the ECC of any of them. The unit test under `$KULA_DISC` holds 423 of them, the 85 pack sectors, the volume descriptor, the twelve directory sectors and 325 sampled every 97th sector across the image, and asserts the count. The viewer's port, `public/js/format/sectors.js`, is the same arithmetic over `Uint8Array`s and is held to the Python side through `tests/fixtures/sectors.json`: thirteen synthetic sectors at varied addresses and submodes and two with a user byte flipped, written by the tool only after its own check against the disc has passed, and carrying no byte of the disc. An emulator passes a sector on whatever its parity says and a console's drive does not, which is why every sector the tools write is rebuilt whole: `tools/kula_warp.py` reads each sector a write touches, lays the bytes into its user data and writes it back with the EDC and ECC recomputed, as the disc has them.

## The pack, written back

A pack is written back as the disc lays it out: the count, the table of offsets and sizes, the name offsets, which start at `4 + 12n` for a pack of `n` records, the names, each ending in a newline before its NUL, one to four bytes of padding to the next four-byte boundary, and the records back to back in slot order, the file ending where the last record ends. The padding is the one thing the disc fills with leftovers, a few bytes of a name's text on some packs and zeros on others, and nothing reads it, so the writer pads with zeros; with that one allowance, `kula_pak.pack` reproduces every pack of the disc from its names and records, and the unit tests hold it to all 21.

The game never reads a name, so what a slot is called is for the next reader of the pack. A level placed in its slot's own bytes keeps the slot's name, since nothing else of the pack moves. A pack rebuilt or moved names the slot after the level's title, in upper-case ASCII and no longer than the name it replaces, or keeps the old name where the title gives nothing; the length rule keeps the name table from growing, so that how much room a slot has is a matter of the records alone and a fit worked out from the table's sizes is never optimistic.

## Placing a level

A level arrives as a zlib stream of the file [editor.md](editor.md) writes, deflated at level 6 as the disc's own streams are, and goes into a slot by the first of three paths that fits, each a reading of how the game reads a pack. The game loads a pack by its directory record's size, reading `(size >> 11) + 1` sectors into a 32,768-byte buffer, and takes a record from the buffer by the offset and size the table gives, so a record's bytes beyond its size and a pack's bytes beyond its size are never read.

**In place**, when the stream is no longer than the record it replaces: the stream is written over the record's first bytes, the table's size for the slot is set, and nothing else moves, not the other records, not the names, not the directory. The bytes the old record had past the new one are left as they were. The room is what the slot had over; LEVEL 1, 299 bytes on the disc, comes back from its fields at 292 and goes in with seven to spare.

**Rebuilt**, when the pack laid out again fits the sectors it has: first with the other records as they are, then, when that overflows, with the other records recompressed at level 9, which the game's inflater takes like any other zlib stream and which wins the bytes in the table below, 505 to 951 a world pack. The rebuilt pack is written over the pack's own sectors with zeros to the end of the last, as the disc's slack is, and the directory record's size is rewritten in both byte orders where it changed. A rebuilt pack carries the game's other levels, recompressed where that is what made room, so a patch of it carries them too. The sectors written are the ones the pack then occupies, which are the sectors it had but in one case: a shorter name can take the table under a sector boundary, and the pack then ends a sector early, with the sector past its new end left as it was, named by nothing, as a moved pack's old sectors are.

**Moved**, when even that does not fit: the pack is written into the first run of zero sectors in `/3.00`, the one file on the disc whose sectors are free, that holds it with a clear sector on either side, and the directory record's extent and size are rewritten. The sector after the pack is kept clear, since the game reads one sector past a pack whose size is a multiple of 2048, and the sector before it where the run is not the head of the file, so that a second pack moves in behind the first and neither reads into the other; the move is refused where no such run is left. A pack is never longer than 32,767 bytes on any path, the most the buffer takes with the sector the game reads over.

The slack is the last sector's, as above; level 9 wins what recompressing every record of the pack at level 9 saves, 8,168 bytes across the disc; and the room for a level is the longest stream a slot takes without a move, the pack's capacity less the table, the names and the other records at level 9, smallest for the slot whose record is smallest. A stream no longer than the slot's record goes in place, one no longer than the record plus the slack rebuilds the pack as it is, one within the room rebuilds it recompressed, and a longer one moves the pack. Written back from their fields, 224 of the disc's 230 levels go into their own slot in place; six come out a few bytes longer than the disc's stream, BONUS 30 by 82 since its pickups are renumbered, and rebuild their pack, LEVEL 33 recompressing INCA's, which has no slack at all.

**Several levels** go onto the disc as one patch. Each is placed over the image as the earlier ones left it, in the order given, so two levels in one pack go in place where each fits its own slot's bytes, and otherwise the later one rebuilds the pack carrying the earlier one's new stream, recompressed with the others where that is what makes room; a level placed in place keeps its slot's name even when a later placement renames another slot; and a pack moved once may be rebuilt in its new place or moved again. The sectors of every placement are merged, a later one replacing an earlier at the same address, so a directory sector that two packs share carries both records and the set is one list of sectors for a patch to carry; two levels naming one slot are refused. The result is read back as one placement's is: each pack touched lies where the last placement into it put it and reads back as that placement's records and names, every level's slot inflates to its level, and every merged sector checks. The fixture below holds two sets beside its single placements, two levels in one pack and a level in a world pack with another in its finals pack, each set with its placements, its merged sectors and the digests of the patched image, the PPF and the VCDIFF.

Everything the fit rests on is in `public/packs.json`, which `tools/kula_build.py` writes from the disc beside `map_data.json`: under `room` the path, sector and size of `/3.00`, and under `packs`, in the order the levels come, each pack's `path`, `lba`, `size` as its directory record gives it, `capacity` as its sectors hold, and its `slots` in order, each with the `name` the table carries, the `size` of the record as stored and `size9`, its length recompressed at level 9. The viewer's `public/js/format/pak.js` lays a pack out from its names and sizes exactly as `kula_pak.pack` does, which is the file's length on all 21 packs since the disc's packs have no gaps, and fits a stream's length to a slot by the three paths above from that file alone, naming the slot after the level's title as a rebuild or a move will, so the fit the page shows without the image is the fit the tools make with it, path and bytes to spare alike, wherever the image is the disc's. Several levels into one pack are fitted in order over a copy of the pack's layout that each fit leaves as the placement will, the slot's record at its stream's length and at its level-9 length once a later rebuild recompresses the others, every other record at its level-9 length after a rebuild that recompressed them, the pack's size and sectors after a rebuild or a move, and the slot's name from the title, so the fit shown for a set is the set's. It can promise no more than that: an image whose packs are not the disc's gives another fit when it is read, and that one is the truth. A title that gives nothing keeps the slot's name, and a name is never longer than the one it replaces, so a fit worked out from the shipped names alone is at worst a few bytes short of the room, never over it.

## What changes on the disc

A placement is a list of sectors, each built whole with its address, subheader, EDC and ECC, and written only where it differs from the sector the image has. In place, the sectors holding the table entry and the record change and no other, which for a record in a pack's first sector is one sector. Rebuilt, every sector of the pack changes, since the records after the slot move, and the world's directory sector changes where the size did, by the eight bytes of the record's size in its two byte orders, the sector's own sync, header and subheader kept. Moved, the sectors of the pack in its run of `/3.00` are new, data sectors ending on an end-of-file sector as a pack's are everywhere else on the disc, and the directory sector changes by the extent and the size; the pack's old sectors are left as they were, named by nothing. The placer reads the result back through the same readers that read the disc, the directory walk and the pack reader, inflates the slot and compares it with the level, compares every record and name with what it meant to write, and checks every sector it wrote.

The unit tests drive every path over a synthetic image, built with the disc's structure in 43 sectors: the volume descriptor, the root and one world's directory, `SYSTEM.CNF`, a world pack and a finals pack of one sector each holding small levels written by the level writer, the world pack's at level 1 so that recompression wins room, a `/3.00` of twelve zero sectors and a postgap; under `$KULA_DISC` they do the same on a copy of the disc, placing LEVEL 1's own stream and larger levels by every path into HIRO's first slot, a longer one into INCA's and two levels at once into HIRO's first two slots, and prove after each that nothing outside the written sectors moved. The image is known by its structure and never by a hash: a raw image of 2352-byte sectors with the volume descriptor at sector 16, the system identifier `PLAYSTATION`, a `SYSTEM.CNF` booting `cdrom:SLUS_007.24;1` and the 21 packs by path, so that a size or a file that is not the disc's is a warning rather than a refusal.

## The patch

A placement's sectors are what a patch carries, in either of two forms, each worked out against the original image. Both describe the changed stretches alone, a stretch being a maximal run of bytes that differ between the original and the patched image, so the sync and header of a written sector, which never change, are left out, as is any byte that happens to agree.

**PPF 3.0** is the PlayStation convention. The file opens with `PPF30`, the version byte 2, fifty bytes of description padded with spaces, which here is `Kula World Map: ` and the level's title, or the titles of several joined by commas, cut to fit, the image type 0 for an image of 2352-byte sectors, the block check flag 1, the undo flag 0 and a zero; then the block check, the 1024 bytes of the original image at offset `0x9320`, 32 bytes into sector 16, which is the volume descriptor from its system identifier `PLAYSTATION` on; then the records, each an eight-byte little-endian offset into the image, a one-byte length from 1 to 255 and that many bytes, one record per changed stretch and a stretch longer than 255 bytes split into several. LEVEL 1 placed back in its own slot is a patch of 1,687 bytes; LEVEL 4 placed in LEVEL 1's slot, which rebuilds the pack, is one of 14,879, most of it the pack's other levels moved.

**VCDIFF**, the format of xdelta, is written as xdelta3 decodes it, with the header `D6 C3 C4 00` and a header indicator of 0, so no secondary compression, no code table and no application header, then one window per 8 MiB of the image, 31 for the disc. A window is the indicator 5, source and checksum, the length and position of the source segment, which is the window's own stretch of the original, the length of the delta encoding, the target window's length, a delta indicator of 0, the lengths of the data, instruction and address sections, the Adler-32 of the window's target bytes as four bytes most significant first, and the three sections. Every integer is base 128, most significant first, with the high bit set on every byte but the last. The instructions are the default code table's: a COPY, opcode 19 with its size following and its address in the address section as an absolute offset into the source segment, for every unchanged stretch, and an ADD, opcode 1 with its size following and its bytes in the data section, for every changed one, so a window with nothing changed is one COPY of itself. xdelta3 checks the Adler-32 of every window and that the delta encoding's length counts everything after it, and the encoding is one that xdelta3 and Rom Patcher JS decode; LEVEL 1 in place is 1,469 bytes of it, LEVEL 4 in LEVEL 1's slot 14,329. The unit tests apply the PPF with an applier of their own and decode the VCDIFF with a decoder that knows COPY and ADD, and both give the image the placer wrote, byte for byte, over windows cut at a sector's middle too; xdelta3 decodes them as well where it is installed. `tests/fixtures/patch-digests.json`, written by `tools/kula_patch.py --fixture`, holds the synthetic image's digest and, for a fixed level placed by each path and for the two sets above, the digests of the patched image, the PPF and the VCDIFF, so the viewer's port can be held to the same bytes.

The viewer carries the same placer and the same writers, in `public/js/format/iso.js`, `public/js/format/pak.js` and `public/js/format/patch.js`: the directory walk over the image a browser is handed, read a slice at a time so that no more of it is in memory than a run of sectors or a window; the pack read and laid out again; the three paths, every touched sector built whole by `public/js/format/sectors.js` and written only where it differs; several levels placed in order, each planned over the patched image as the earlier ones left it, their sectors merged with a later one replacing an earlier at the same address, and read back pack by pack as the tools read a set back; and the PPF, the VCDIFF and the patched image, which is the original's own slices with the written sectors between them, so the image is never held whole. What holds them to the tools is the fixture above: the unit tests build the synthetic image from the level writer up and it has to carry the fixture's digest, each path's placement has to write the same sectors, leave the same room and give the patched image, the PPF and the VCDIFF their fixture digests, byte for byte, with the PPF applied and the VCDIFF decoded in the tests as they are on the Python side, and each of the fixture's sets has to come out as the tools placed it, placement by placement and merged sector by merged sector, with the same three digests.

**How an emulator applies a PPF.** DuckStation reads a patch named after the image it loads, with `.ppf` in place of the extension, from the image's own directory, and only while Apply Image Patches in its console settings is on, which it is not by default; it lays each record over the sector it falls in and leaves EDC and ECC as the patch has them. PCSX-Redux reads the same file by the same name with nothing to turn on. ePSXe and PCSX-ReARMed read it from their patches folder under the game's serial, `SLUS_007.24`, with no extension. Mednafen, Beetle and RetroArch apply no patch to a disc image and take the patched copy instead, which plays everywhere and can be burned, since every sector it carries is built whole.
