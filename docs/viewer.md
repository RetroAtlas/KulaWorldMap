# The viewer

What the map under `public/` does for whoever is looking at it, control by control, and how it is built. The readings it draws are in [level-format.md](level-format.md), [tgi.md](tgi.md), [ggi.md](ggi.md) and [motion.md](motion.md), the start camera it can draw in [camera.md](camera.md), and the names it shows in [annotations.md](annotations.md).

## Turning, panning and zooming

**Drag to turn the level.** A lattice has no side that is the right one to look from, so dragging orbits the camera around it rather than sliding the view: left and right swing around, up and down raise and lower the eye between looking along the floor and looking straight down. `q` and `e` snap the swing to 45°. A level opens framed to fit the window and keeps fitting it as the window changes, until you turn, pan or zoom it or go to a find; `f`, or the button at the map's bottom right, frames it again, and the button is dim for as long as the level stays framed. Once a search has centred the view on a find, dragging turns about the find for as long as it stays selected; `Esc` clears it and the level is back under the hand, with nothing on screen moving until the next drag.

**Pan** with shift-drag, a right-drag, two fingers, or the arrow keys. If you would rather drag panned all the time, `p` switches a plain drag between panning and turning.

**Zoom** with the wheel, a pinch, or `+` / `-`, anchored wherever the pointer is.

## The slice, the textures and the lines

**Slice** the level with `,` and `.`, which lower and raise a ceiling so you can see inside a stack. `\` puts the whole level back, and `h` leaves what the slice takes off on the map, faint.

`t` puts the game's own block textures on and off; with them off, and until a world's textures arrive, the lattice draws in the world's own colour, the mean of the textures its plain blocks wear, which the swatch beside the world in the sidebar shows too. `b` puts on the lines the map draws over the blocks, none of which the game draws: their edges, the broken outline of a block that is not solidly there, the route a moving platform runs, and a laser's beam while it is switched off. `g` lays a faint grid under the level's lowest layer.

## The panel

**Click** a block or an object for what the game stores about it: its lattice cell, its kind and type, and the raw fields the record carries, each by its word in the slot and, where what it holds is known, by name. Where a click has another meaning, a right click selects all the same, and on a touch screen so does a long press. `Esc` clears. A switch's panel has a button that presses it, as the ball would: every laser, teleporter and switch of its colour turns on or off, and what travels finds the beams' cells open or shut, until you leave the level. The panel of an exit, or of a hidden exit, has one that opens every exit of the level as taking its last key would, and shuts them again, until you leave the level: open, an exit is green and turns faster, the cross on its face is green, and from 19 frames on it casts its light as a device that is on does, and a hidden exit, which the map draws turning slowly all along where the game keeps it out of sight until the last key, turns faster too. A level with no key has no last key to take, and no button. A teleporter's panel names the one it sends the ball to, which the map draws an arrow to while it is selected, looping where one stands in front of the other, broken while it is switched off, and a click on the name goes there as a find does, so where three or more share a colour the names lead round their ring. A teleporter's facing is the way the ball faces as it comes out of it.

The panel folds to its title line with the chevron beside its cross, and stays folded from block to block until it is opened again.

## The legend

The sidebar's "On this level" lists what the level holds, by name and count: the objects first, then the blocks and the faces, which are the kinds of block the level uses and the things that are paint on a face rather than standing on it, fire, ice and the clock among them. A row with a button hides and shows its kind on the map, and with it everything the kind shows, the light a device casts and the paint it puts on its face included; shift-click shows that kind alone, and again shows all of them; the heading's reset puts every kind back; a kind of block's row only counts.

## The compass

The block at the map's bottom right is a compass, turned as the level is and shaded as the map shades a face, with the textures or without, so the brightest side on it is the brightest on the level. It names each face turned to you on the block, a side by the way it faces (`+x`, `-y`) and the top and the underside by name, and beyond the block, where each way points, every way it leaves unnamed, faint where the way points away from you: `-z` is up and `+z` down, since `z` counts downward. These are the words the panel and the search use, for the side a thing stands on (`on the +x side`) and the way it faces (`facing=-y`).

## The objects

`d` switches the objects between the game's own meshes and markers, a diamond with its type number off the face it stands on, and `o` hides them, and with them the shadow the game paints into the face under a pickup; what a device, an exit or the clock paints on its face stays. The level's settings have no mesh, so among the meshes their marker shows only on the selected block. A thing on a face turned away from you is hidden by its own block wherever the block covers it, as in play, and `x` shows what the blocks cover faintly through them; what stands on the selected block always shows through. `l` labels each object by its name, and `n` adds the face it stands on.

## What moves

What moves in play moves here, as the game moves it: the platforms, the stars, the wheel and the wandering ball travel, what stands in place turns, bobs, tilts and breathes, the spikes, the vanishing blocks, the fire and the beams run their cycles, a gem's facets flash white and fade back a few at a time, the same facets on every gem of its colour, a teleporter or a switch that is on, or an exit opened from the panel, casts a light of its colour over the faces round it and brightens with it, on and off every third of a second, and a boost button sinks under the ball, the pointer and the selection, beside which an invisible block lights up as it does beside the ball, or on the few levels that show them from afar, fades away. `v` switches Motion off and holds the level still, every thing where and as the level starts it, except that the moving spikes stand up, a vanishing block shows as it does when it starts to go, there and seen through, and a beam and an invisible block shine at their brightest, the invisible block unlit. Switching it on again starts the level afresh. A system set to reduce motion opens the map with it off.

## The switches and Settings

A switch that does something only while another is on is greyed while that one is off, and keeps its tick for when it comes back: the models, seeing through the blocks and the labels need the objects on, the face in the labels needs the labels, and dimming what the slice hides needs the slice lowered. Its key, or a click or tap on it, then leaves it as it is and says for a moment at the foot of the map what it needs.

The gear at the top of the sidebar, or `s`, opens Settings, for the switches used less, their keys working from the map as the others do and in the panel itself. The Display's reset leaves them as they are. Among them, `c` draws where the game's camera stands as the level opens, behind the ball and above it, and the wedge it sees ([camera.md](camera.md)), and the compass at the map's bottom right can be put away; under Play, the Down key can be given the turn about while the ball is rolled, which the game's own pad has no button for, the pad on a touch screen taking a down arrow with it, the way the ball has come can be drawn under it, off by default since the game draws no trail, the golden dust the ball leaves, the map's and not the game's either, can be put away, and a block's panel can be given Play from here, off by default so that the map opens as a map, which stands the ball on the face you clicked and plays from there. `m` shows and hides the sidebar itself.

## Search

**Search** with `/` for a level (`level 42`, `bonus`, `final 7`), a world (`inca`), a cell (`17,12,17`), an object by name or type (`key`, `type 34`), or a block by the name or number of its kind (`ice block`, `kind=2`), where `block` alone finds every block named for its kind and none of the plain ones. A search for objects or blocks lists every one the game places, this level's first, then the rest of its world's, then the other worlds'; Enter or a click goes to one and selects it, and the list stays for the next. The cross in the box clears it, as `Esc` does. A space means every term must match and a comma or `or` means any (`coin, key`), and a field is searched as `name=value` (`starts=off`, `facing=+x`, or `type 34 f10=2` for the raw fields the panel shows). The bar under the box narrows a search to the world or the level in hand, or, by level, lists the levels that hold the thing with the one holding most of it first.

## The level line and the order of the levels

The line at the top of the map names the level and counts what it holds, with the level's note under it where there is one. A click or a tap on the line folds the note away and back, a dot on the line saying there is one, and the map keeps it as it was left, from level to level and from one visit to the next. On a level of your own the note is the level's overview instead, editing or not: how it plays, as an arcade level with a key or a bonus level without, hidden or not, in the sentence the Level card also says, the chip spreading to the map's width on a phone so that it takes fewer rows; and, under one line that counts the errors and the warnings and says the counts are below it, its counts against the room the game has, every one that could be counted, those at nought dimmed, red where one is over it and amber where it is more than any level on the disc, and everything the checks find, errors first, each at a cell a click on it selects, so the panel is right there, a fold that stands open where the sidebar has a column of its own and closed on a phone, where the sidebar overlays the map, until it is opened, and then stays as it was last left, from draft to draft, for the rest of the visit. It is brought up to date at every change, a link that holds the focus keeping it, and folds as any note does. One finding is the map's own rather than the file's: where a key, a coin or anything else the ball takes stands where no roll from the start reaches it, over the blocks alone as the planner reckons a route, the note names each with the face and the block it stands on, the keys first, a face that looks into another block left to the checks, and says that jumps, teleporters, moving platforms, ice and a fall from a crumbling or vanishing block are not counted.

The sidebar lists a world's levels in the order of its pack, then its two finals, then the Simon room the game's Simon mode plays in it on its first round, and `[` and `]` step to the previous and next level in that order, on from a world's last level to the next world's first and back, and stop at the first level of the first world and the last of the last.

## Your levels

The sidebar's "Your levels", after the worlds, lists the levels of your own this browser holds, folded under its heading as the worlds are, with how many it holds and a chevron beside the name: the fold is closed on a first visit, opens by itself when a level of your own is made, copied, opened or followed to, and otherwise stays as it was last left, across visits and reloads. "Make a level" at the top of the sidebar does what `w` does; the editor behind them loads only when it is wanted, when a level of your own is made, opened or linked to, or, once the map has drawn its first level, when this browser already holds one, so a plain visit loads none of its code, though the strip's and the palette's markup and the editor's styles are in every page, hidden until they are wanted. `new` makes a level of one plain block and a start in the world on screen and opens it editing, and `copy this level` makes one out of the level on screen, a shipped level as the game loads it or a copy of a draft, and opens it as it is; either is a draft at once, marked "Draft" on the level line and shown on the map as the game would load it. A row shows a draft, with "not saved to a file" and, once there have been changes, how many, undo taking one away and redo giving it back and a typed title counting once; choosing one shows it, as choosing a level does, and a link to it (`#DRAFT/3/...`) opens it on the device that holds it, pasted into the address bar as well as followed at the start, and anywhere else gives way to the view on screen, saying once that the level is kept in another browser. Where this browser holds levels of its own that could not be read, a row under the drafts counts them and offers to remove them, which asks to be pressed again; they are otherwise kept as they are, and said once, the first time a tab finds them, rather than at every reload. For the shown draft a Level card edits its title, up to eighty characters, its world, the time its clock starts at, any number of seconds from 1 to 32767, rounded to whole seconds, which says so when it keeps the old value, and whether it plays as a hidden level or shows invisible blocks from afar, each change redrawing the map with the view, the slice, the hidden kinds, the selection, the clock and the switch presses kept; a greyed Bonus level box above Hidden level is ticked while the level has no key, since the game decides that from the keys alone, and its label says why, pressed, pointed at or read; under those, Circuits lists the four colours, each with its swatch, whether it starts on or off, which its button turns over as one step undo takes back, and how many switches, teleporters and lasers of it the level has, or that it has none, so a colour's starting state is set beside the level's other settings, each row a group named Circuit, as the panel's is, and each button named for a reader by its colour, "green circuit", with whether it starts on in whether it is pressed, since "starts on" is the state and not the name, and the swatch there for the eye alone, and the card announces none of its changes to the level's settings, where the panel does, so a change made from it clears what the panel last said; under the settings the card says how the level plays, as the level line's note does, and, when the checks find errors, how many and that the level line's note lists them; and it offers `duplicate` and `delete`, which asks to be pressed again, each saying what it did and which level is then on screen. The card's `save to a file` writes the draft as the map's own file and `save as the game's level` as the file the game loads, each named after the title, after which the row reads "saved to a file" and counts the changes since, with a dot on the save button while there are any; `open a file…` in the row above takes either back, or a level file from elsewhere, read for what it holds rather than by its name, opens it as a new draft and says what it had to repair, or that it has notes on what it read where it repaired nothing; and on a shipped level `save as the game's level` writes that level's own file. `open a file…` also takes a pack of the game's, which asks which of its levels to open and opens it in the world on screen, since a pack does not say which world it is from, a disc image, which asks for the pack and the level and opens it in the world the map lists the level that lies there under, named as the pause screen names it, and in the pack's own world where none of the game's lies there, and a level saved by the community's level viewer, read in the world on screen; declining the question opens nothing. `paste a link…` in the same row opens a box for the link to a level shared from the community's level viewer, which opens as a new draft in the world on screen, or in the world the link names where that is one of the ten; a text that is no such link is said so under the field, which stays for another try. Your levels stay in this browser, written a moment after each change, with "Saved in this browser" shown for a moment on the card, until they are saved to a file; a browser that cannot keep them, one that gives the map no room for them, one with no room for more, or one that refuses to keep them for another reason, says so.

A draft goes onto the disc from its Level card, which opens "Put it on the disc", five steps in one box. The level: its title, how it plays, and what the checks find, the errors listed with the sentence that a level with errors cannot go on the disc until they are fixed, the warnings folded away. Where it goes: a slot of any of the ten worlds' packs or their finals packs, named as the pause screen names the level there and grouped by world, the Simon rooms left out, with how the game reaches that slot, a new game starting at LEVEL 1 and the rest taking a save that has got that far or a world finished, and a note that the level will wear that world's artwork where it is not the draft's own; the choice stays with the draft and in its file. The fit: whether the level fits the slot's own bytes, needs the pack laid out again, with the other levels packed tighter where that is what makes room, or moves the pack into the disc's free sectors, with the bytes to spare, worked out from the disc as pressed until the image is given and read from the image after, anything about the image that is not the disc's said as a warning rather than a refusal. The disc: the image, dropped onto the box or chosen, read in the browser and sent nowhere, and then the downloads, a PPF patch, an xdelta patch, and the patched disc with its cue sheet, each named after the level, the disc saved through the browser's own picker where it has one. Playing it: where each emulator wants the patch, DuckStation and PCSX-Redux beside the image under its name, ePSXe and PCSX-ReARMed in their patches folder under the game's serial, how xdelta3 applies the other, and that Mednafen, Beetle and RetroArch take the patched disc instead. A browser that cannot unpack the game's files says a newer one is needed. Before any download is offered the plan is read back through the patched disc, the directory, the pack and the slot read as the game reads them and every written sector checked, and the patches are then the stretches where those sectors differ from the image, so a browser that cannot put that disc together is told the level cannot be placed and gets no patch. The box follows the draft while it is open: when another tab's copy of the level arrives, the checks, the fit and the downloads are worked out again for it, and when another tab deletes the draft the box closes and says so.

## The editor

On a draft, `w` or the strip's Edit button turns editing on and off, and on a shipped level `w` makes a copy and opens it editing in one step; choosing a shipped level turns it off, and editing is never in the link, never stored and not a Display switch. The strip at the map's bottom left holds the tools: Place (`a`) lays the brush's block in the cell across the face you click, a plain block until a palette row is pressed, Erase (`z`) takes what stands on the face you click, or the block with everything on it where nothing does, a platform or a laser whole, Select (`i`) is the viewer's own click, with a rectangle for several blocks at once, below, and Turn (`r`) turns the selected thing a quarter turn where its kind faces, or else what the next placement will face, Shift-`r` the other way; shift-click erases and a right click selects from any tool, and `Esc` clears the selection without leaving the mode. While Place or Erase is the tool the face under the pointer is washed and outlined, Place stands a dashed ghost in the cell it would fill, red where the click would be refused, and the tip's last line says what it would place and on which side, or that the side holds it already; with the Ground grid on the grid spans the whole floor under the level, the ghost follows the pointer across it and a click lays a block there, so a first floor has somewhere to land; while the slice is lowered the grid stands at the slice instead, under the topmost layer still shown, and a click lays a block in that layer, so `,` and `.` choose the height a floor is built at, which the strip says the first time the grid is used that way, and a level with no blocks has its grid at the lattice's foot. The game builds nothing past the edge, a cell cannot be filled twice or across a platform's run or a laser's beam, a face is not given what it holds already, and a level keeps its last block: each refusal is said at the foot of the strip, where it stays until the next change or a change of tool or brush clears it, and changes nothing, a block laid above the slice raises the slice to show it, and what was laid is selected, so the panel is right there. Every step can be taken back: `Ctrl-Z` (or `Cmd-Z`) undoes the last change to the draft on screen and `Ctrl-Shift-Z` or `Ctrl-Y` takes it again, as the strip's Undo and Redo do, a new step after an undo lets the steps taken back go, a change that leaves the draft as it stands is no step and counts as no change, letters typed into the title within a second of each other join one step, so a pause starts another and a run of them that ends where it began leaves none, and `Delete` or `Backspace` erases what is selected, its object first and then its block, or every block of a selected set; the steps are this visit's alone, kept for each draft until the page is left, and a form field keeps the browser's own keys.

With Select, a drag that begins on no block draws a rectangle on the screen, and the blocks whose middles lie in it when the mouse is let go are selected together, a platform or a laser whole when any cell of its run is, each outlined, and the strip says how many are selected and what can be done with them; what the slice has taken off stays out of it, and the panel keeps the block last clicked, which a rectangle round nothing clears along with the set. Dragging one of the set moves it whole: it follows the pointer across the floor at its own height, a cell at a time, or with Shift held as the drag begins it rises and sinks instead, a cell for each cell's height the pointer moves, and the set's ghosts stand where it would land, red where a block would land past the edge or on a block that is not moving, or where the game's rules refuse the result, in which case letting go says why and moves nothing; what stands on the blocks travels with them, the panel's block goes with the set where it is one of them, and the move is one step for undo. With a set selected, Shift with the arrow keys moves it a cell along the two ground axes nearest the screen's directions, as the compass names them, Shift-PageUp and Shift-PageDown a cell up and down, `Delete` or `Backspace` erases every block of the set in one step, a level keeping its last block, and `Esc` clears the set before anything else it does; a click on a block selects that block alone and clears the set, as a rectangle round nothing does, changing the tool, leaving editing, or undoing or redoing a step clears it too, and a drag that begins on a block outside the set turns the level as ever, so a set is always begun from empty space. A set is made with a mouse or a pen: a finger's drag turns the level, so there is no rectangle and no dragging a set by touch, though once a set is made the keys work on it there as anywhere.

The palette floats above the strip while editing, and the strip's Palette button puts it away and brings it back. Its tabs are the groups a player meets, blocks, what is paint on a face or the ball's start, the devices, the hazards and the pickups, and its rows are every kind of block and every thing the game places, by name and with the legend's icon, a teleporter or a switch of each colour, a coin of each worth and a gem of each colour a row of their own, the fruit as one row since the game shows the player's next fruit whichever is placed, and the one thing the game never names by its number. The pressed row is the brush: a block makes Place lay that kind, and a thing makes Place put it on the face you click in place of whatever stood there, so the tip's last line names it and its icon stands on the hovered face. `1` to `9` press the shown group's rows and `j` and `k` step through every row across the groups; Alt-click takes whatever is under the pointer as the brush, the thing on the face with its colour and its facing, or the kind of the block. A teleporter, a switch, an exit, a hidden exit or moving spikes work on a plain block only, nothing stands on a crumbling or vanishing block's faces, and a face that looks into another block takes nothing: each is said and refused. Placing a thing of a kind the legend hides brings the kind back.

A moving platform or a laser is a run, laid in two clicks. The first sets the run's own block where a block would be laid, against the face you click or on the floor through the grid, and the strip says the run waits for its far end, telling a finger to tap where it ends; the pointer then previews the run from that block to the cell you hover, or to the grid's cell under the pointer, straight along whichever way it leaves the block by most, the earlier of `x`, `y` and `z` where two tie, every cell tinted where the run may go and red where it may not; the second click lays it, the far end across the face you click, on the floor through the grid, or, for a laser, on the plain bare block or the other laser's block you click, which a laser may end on. A run keeps to one line and to the lattice, a platform's run stays empty, a laser's two ends are each empty, a plain bare block or another laser's with nothing between, and a run needs two cells: each refusal is said and lays nothing, and `Esc` cancels a waiting run, as changing the tool or the brush does. The palette's laser rows come in the four colours and Alt-click on a laser takes its colour; a click with a platform or laser brush on a run's own block takes that block as the first click, whatever the brush's kind or colour, so the second re-lays its run in one step and the run keeps its own kind and colour; Erase on a run's block takes the run whole; and nothing stands on a platform's or a laser's faces.

While editing, the panel that opens on a selected block of the draft is also its form, standing above the block's own rows, which fold under "Block information" and stay open or folded as they were last left, from block to block. A row of six buttons, Faces, stands for the block's faces, a dot on each that holds something, the chosen one pressed and a face that looks into another block greyed and saying which; choosing one selects it, as a click on the map does. Under it the block's kind can be changed, a vanishing block's phase set, a platform's length and speed typed and a laser's colour chosen, the axis and the far end of a run shown as they are, and a run's other cells show the run's own block; what the chosen face holds is a list grouped as the palette is, with nothing at its head, and the thing on it can be faced one of its four ways, under Facing, given its colour, worth or phase, and a teleporter says where it leads. A face that holds a switch or a teleporter, and a laser's block, show the circuit of their colour in one row: its swatch, whether it starts on or off, which the button turns over, and how many switches, teleporters and lasers share the colour, the row the Level card shows for all four, read as the card's is, the button named by its colour and the swatch for the eye alone. "Place the brush here" and "Build against this face" lay what a click on that face would, so a level can be built from the keyboard; the Place button is greyed while the face holds what it would place, keeping the focus and saying so when pressed, and with a platform or a laser for the brush there is none, since a run is laid on the map in two clicks. The panel keeps to the block and its face: how the level plays, its counts and what the checks find are the level line's note, so they are read once rather than on every block. Every change is one step that undo takes back, a change the game would not forgive is refused and put back, as the tools refuse it, a Length or a Speed that is not a number is put back and says so, an emptied one, or one that rounds to what the block holds, in silence, and the field being worked keeps the focus while the map redraws.

On a touch screen there is no hover, so Place and Erase take two taps: the first tap on a face marks it as the hover would, with the wash, the ghost or the brush's icon and a line on the strip saying what a second tap does, and the second tap on the same face does it, though a first tap on a face that holds what the brush would put there says so and marks nothing; a tap elsewhere marks that face instead, and a drag or a pinch, which turn and pan and zoom as ever, marks nothing. A run takes two such pairs. A long press selects whatever is under the finger from any tool, as a right click does with a mouse. On a phone the palette and the panel open as sheets at the foot of the map, one at a time, each with a close button, the strip standing above whichever is open and the map still turning above that; the strip's Palette button opens the palette, which does not open of itself there, and `Esc` or the close button puts a sheet away, `Esc` taking the palette's before it clears the selection, which takes the panel's with it. What a tap places or clears is selected and outlined as with a mouse, but the panel's sheet stays down, through a change of brush or of mode too, and through every redraw of the draft, a second block, an erase, an undo or a card edit, and the palette's stays as it was, so a floor is built without a sheet to put away at every block; a long press, or a tap with Select, opens the panel.

## Planning a route

`u`, or Route planner among the Display switches, turns the planner on. While it is on, a click or a tap on a block's face adds a stop there, and the stops are joined by a golden route drawn over the faces, a chevron at every roll but one that runs back over a roll already drawn, that reads from the first stop to the last; the first click on a level that has a start begins the route there, heading the way the ball faces as the level opens, so the route starts where the game does. A right click, or a long press on a touch screen, opens a block's panel as it does with the planner off. A stop one roll from the last is joined by that roll; one further off is joined by the shortest way the ball could roll there by the game's own rules for a step, over the faces, up a wall where a block stands ahead, round an edge where one ends and nothing stands beside it, turning at the corners as the ball turns; and where no roll reaches a stop, a dashed leap joins it to the last. The route knows the blocks and nothing of what stands on them or of ice, so it runs through a laser's beam and over spikes as readily as over a coin, and what it says is where the blocks let the ball go, not whether the ball would live or how long it would take. A face that looks into another block, or a moving platform's block, takes no stop and says so, and though the shortest way may run over a platform at rest, the route is not drawn across it. A click on the last stop takes it back, as Backspace does while nothing is selected, and the strip at the map's bottom left counts the stops and the leaps, takes the last stop back, clears the route, which asks to be pressed again, and copies a link that carries it; on a level of your own it stands above the editor's strip, and while editing the editor's tools take the clicks, the strip keeping only its line that says to leave editing to plan, and on a phone put away until editing ends. The route is kept with its level in this browser, for levels of your own as well, and it rides in the link, so a link copied with it opens with it wherever it is opened; a link without one leaves the kept route as it is. The switch itself is not kept from one visit to the next, and a route stays on the map with the switch off. A level of your own edited under a route keeps the route as it was: a stop whose face no longer stands is a dashed ring and a roll the blocks no longer allow a dashed leap, so an undo of the edit makes the route whole again. While the ball is on the level, Follow the route on the play strip rolls it along the route, as Rolling the ball below says.
