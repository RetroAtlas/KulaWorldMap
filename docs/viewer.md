# The viewer

What the map under `public/` does for whoever is looking at it, control by control, and how it is built. The readings it draws are in [level-format.md](level-format.md), [tgi.md](tgi.md), [ggi.md](ggi.md) and [motion.md](motion.md), the start camera it can draw in [camera.md](camera.md), and the names it shows in [annotations.md](annotations.md).

## Turning, panning and zooming

**Drag to turn the level.** A lattice has no side that is the right one to look from, so dragging orbits the camera around it rather than sliding the view: left and right swing around, up and down raise and lower the eye between looking along the floor and looking straight down. `q` and `e` snap the swing to 45°. A level opens framed to fit the window and keeps fitting it as the window changes, until you turn, pan or zoom it or go to a find; `f`, or the button at the map's bottom right, frames it again, and the button is dim for as long as the level stays framed. Once a search has centred the view on a find, dragging turns about the find for as long as it stays selected; `Esc` clears it and the level is back under the hand, with nothing on screen moving until the next drag.

**Pan** with shift-drag, a right-drag, two fingers, or the arrow keys. If you would rather drag panned all the time, `p` switches a plain drag between panning and turning.

**Zoom** with the wheel, a pinch, or `+` / `-`, anchored wherever the pointer is.

## The slice, the textures and the lines

**Slice** the level with `,` and `.`, which lower and raise a ceiling so you can see inside a stack. `\` puts the whole level back, and `h` leaves what the slice takes off on the map, faint.

`t` puts the game's own block textures on and off; with them off, and until a world's textures arrive, the lattice draws in the world's own colour, the mean of the textures its plain blocks wear, which the swatch beside the world in the sidebar shows too. `b` puts on the lines the map draws over the blocks, none of which the game draws: their edges, the broken outline of a block that is not solidly there, the route a moving platform runs, and a laser's beam while it is switched off. `g` lays a faint grid under the level's lowest layer.

## The panel

**Click** a block or an object for what the game stores about it: its lattice cell, its kind and type, and the raw fields the record carries, each by its word in the slot and, where what it holds is known, by name. `Esc` clears. A switch's panel has a button that presses it, as the ball would: every laser, teleporter and switch of its colour turns on or off, and what travels finds the beams' cells open or shut, until you leave the level. The panel of an exit, or of a hidden exit, has one that opens every exit of the level as taking its last key would, and shuts them again, until you leave the level: open, an exit is green and turns faster, the cross on its face is green, and from 19 frames on it casts its light as a device that is on does, and a hidden exit, which the map draws turning slowly all along where the game keeps it out of sight until the last key, turns faster too. A level with no key has no last key to take, and no button. A teleporter's panel names the one it sends the ball to, which the map draws an arrow to while it is selected, looping where one stands in front of the other, broken while it is switched off, and a click on the name goes there as a find does, so where three or more share a colour the names lead round their ring. A teleporter's facing is the way the ball faces as it comes out of it.

The panel folds to its title line with the chevron beside its cross, and stays folded from block to block until it is opened again.

## The legend

The sidebar's "On this level" lists what the level holds, by name and count: the objects first, then the blocks and the faces, which are the kinds of block the level uses and the things that are paint on a face rather than standing on it, fire, ice and the clock among them. A row with a button hides and shows its kind on the map, and with it everything the kind shows, the light a device casts and the paint it puts on its face included; shift-click shows that kind alone, and again shows all of them; the heading's reset puts every kind back; a kind of block's row only counts.

## The compass

The block at the map's bottom right is a compass, turned as the level is and shaded as the map shades a face, with the textures or without, so the brightest side on it is the brightest on the level. It names each face turned to you on the block, a side by the way it faces (`+x`, `-y`) and the top and the underside by name, and beyond the block, where each way points, every way it leaves unnamed, faint where the way points away from you: `-z` is up and `+z` down, since `z` counts downward. These are the words the panel and the search use, for the side a thing stands on (`on the +x side`) and the way it faces (`facing=-y`).

## The objects

`d` switches the objects between the game's own meshes and markers, a diamond with its type number off the face it stands on, and `o` hides them. The level's settings have no mesh, so among the meshes their marker shows only on the selected block. A thing on a face turned away from you is hidden by its own block wherever the block covers it, as in play, and `x` shows what the blocks cover faintly through them; what stands on the selected block always shows through. `l` labels each object by its name, and `n` adds the face it stands on.

## What moves

What moves in play moves here, as the game moves it: the platforms, the stars, the wheel and the wandering ball travel, what stands in place turns, bobs, tilts and breathes, the spikes, the vanishing blocks, the fire and the beams run their cycles, a gem's facets flash white and fade back a few at a time, the same facets on every gem of its colour, a teleporter or a switch that is on, or an exit opened from the panel, casts a light of its colour over the faces round it and brightens with it, on and off every third of a second, and a boost button sinks under the ball, the pointer and the selection, beside which an invisible block lights up as it does beside the ball, or on the few levels that show them from afar, fades away. `v` switches Motion off and holds the level still, every thing where and as the level starts it, except that the moving spikes stand up, a vanishing block shows as it does when it starts to go, there and seen through, and a beam and an invisible block shine at their brightest, the invisible block unlit. Switching it on again starts the level afresh. A system set to reduce motion opens the map with it off.

## The switches and Settings

A switch that does something only while another is on is greyed while that one is off, and keeps its tick for when it comes back: the models, seeing through the blocks and the labels need the objects on, the face in the labels needs the labels, and dimming what the slice hides needs the slice lowered. Its key, or a click or tap on it, then leaves it as it is and says for a moment at the foot of the map what it needs.

The gear at the top of the sidebar, or `s`, opens Settings, for the switches used less, their keys working from the map as the others do and in the panel itself. The Display's reset leaves them as they are. Among them, `c` draws where the game's camera stands as the level opens, behind the ball and above it, and the wedge it sees ([camera.md](camera.md)), and the compass at the map's bottom right can be put away. `m` shows and hides the sidebar itself.

## Search

**Search** with `/` for a level (`level 42`, `bonus`, `final 7`), a world (`inca`), a cell (`17,12,17`), an object by name or type (`key`, `type 34`), or a block by the name or number of its kind (`ice block`, `kind=2`), where `block` alone finds every block named for its kind and none of the plain ones. A search for objects or blocks lists every one the game places, this level's first, then the rest of its world's, then the other worlds'; Enter or a click goes to one and selects it, and the list stays for the next. The cross in the box clears it, as `Esc` does. A space means every term must match and a comma or `or` means any (`coin, key`), and a field is searched as `name=value` (`starts=off`, `facing=+x`, or `type 34 f10=2` for the raw fields the panel shows). The bar under the box narrows a search to the world or the level in hand, or, by level, lists the levels that hold the thing with the one holding most of it first.

## The level line and the order of the levels

The line at the top of the map names the level and counts what it holds, with the level's note under it where there is one. A click or a tap on the line folds the note away and back, a dot on the line saying there is one, and the map keeps it as it was left, from level to level and from one visit to the next. On a level of your own the note is the level's overview instead, editing or not: how it plays, as an arcade level with a key or a bonus level without, hidden or not, in the sentence the Level card also says, the chip spreading to the map's width on a phone so that it takes fewer rows; and, under one line that counts the errors and the warnings and says the counts are below it, its counts against the room the game has, every one that could be counted, those at nought dimmed, red where one is over it and amber where it is more than any level on the disc, and everything the checks find, errors first, each at a cell a click on it selects, so the panel is right there, a fold that stands open where the sidebar has a column of its own and closed on a phone, where the sidebar overlays the map, until it is opened, and then stays as it was last left, from draft to draft, for the rest of the visit. It is brought up to date at every change, a link that holds the focus keeping it, and folds as any note does.

The sidebar lists a world's levels in the order of its pack, then its two finals, then the Simon room the game's Simon mode plays in it on its first round, and `[` and `]` step to the previous and next level in that order, on from a world's last level to the next world's first and back, and stop at the first level of the first world and the last of the last.

## The editor

On a draft, `w` or the strip's Edit button turns editing on and off, and on a shipped level `w` makes a copy and opens it editing in one step; choosing a shipped level turns it off, and editing is never in the link, never stored and not a Display switch. The strip at the map's bottom left holds the tools: Place (`a`) lays the brush's block in the cell across the face you click, a plain block until a palette row is pressed, Erase (`z`) takes what stands on the face you click, or the block with everything on it where nothing does, a platform or a laser whole, Select (`i`) is the viewer's own click, with a rectangle for several blocks at once, below, and Turn (`r`) turns the selected thing a quarter turn where its kind faces, or else what the next placement will face, Shift-`r` the other way; shift-click erases and a right click selects from any tool, and `Esc` clears the selection without leaving the mode. While Place or Erase is the tool the face under the pointer is washed and outlined, Place stands a dashed ghost in the cell it would fill, red where the click would be refused, and the tip's last line says what it would place and on which side, or that the side holds it already; with the Ground grid on the grid spans the whole floor under the level, the ghost follows the pointer across it and a click lays a block there, so a first floor has somewhere to land; while the slice is lowered the grid stands at the slice instead, under the topmost layer still shown, and a click lays a block in that layer, so `,` and `.` choose the height a floor is built at, which the strip says the first time the grid is used that way, and a level with no blocks has its grid at the lattice's foot. The game builds nothing past the edge, a cell cannot be filled twice or across a platform's run or a laser's beam, a face is not given what it holds already, and a level keeps its last block: each refusal is said at the foot of the strip, where it stays until the next change or a change of tool or brush clears it, and changes nothing, a block laid above the slice raises the slice to show it, and what was laid is selected, so the panel is right there. Every step can be taken back: `Ctrl-Z` (or `Cmd-Z`) undoes the last change to the draft on screen and `Ctrl-Shift-Z` or `Ctrl-Y` takes it again, as the strip's Undo and Redo do, a new step after an undo lets the steps taken back go, a change that leaves the draft as it stands is no step and counts as no change, letters typed into the title within a second of each other join one step, so a pause starts another and a run of them that ends where it began leaves none, and `Delete` or `Backspace` erases what is selected, its object first and then its block, or every block of a selected set; the steps are this visit's alone, kept for each draft until the page is left, and a form field keeps the browser's own keys.

With Select, a drag that begins on no block draws a rectangle on the screen, and the blocks whose middles lie in it when the mouse is let go are selected together, a platform or a laser whole when any cell of its run is, each outlined, and the strip says how many are selected and what can be done with them; what the slice has taken off stays out of it, and the panel keeps the block last clicked, which a rectangle round nothing clears along with the set. Dragging one of the set moves it whole: it follows the pointer across the floor at its own height, a cell at a time, or with Shift held as the drag begins it rises and sinks instead, a cell for each cell's height the pointer moves, and the set's ghosts stand where it would land, red where a block would land past the edge or on a block that is not moving, or where the game's rules refuse the result, in which case letting go says why and moves nothing; what stands on the blocks travels with them, the panel's block goes with the set where it is one of them, and the move is one step for undo. With a set selected, Shift with the arrow keys moves it a cell along the two ground axes nearest the screen's directions, as the compass names them, Shift-PageUp and Shift-PageDown a cell up and down, `Delete` or `Backspace` erases every block of the set in one step, a level keeping its last block, and `Esc` clears the set before anything else it does; a click on a block selects that block alone and clears the set, as a rectangle round nothing does, changing the tool, leaving editing, or undoing or redoing a step clears it too, and a drag that begins on a block outside the set turns the level as ever, so a set is always begun from empty space. A set is made with a mouse or a pen: a finger's drag turns the level, so there is no rectangle and no dragging a set by touch, though once a set is made the keys work on it there as anywhere.

The palette floats above the strip while editing, and the strip's Palette button puts it away and brings it back. Its tabs are the groups a player meets, blocks, what is paint on a face or the ball's start, the devices, the hazards and the pickups, and its rows are every kind of block and every thing the game places, by name and with the legend's icon, a teleporter or a switch of each colour, a coin of each worth and a gem of each colour a row of their own, the fruit as one row since the game shows the player's next fruit whichever is placed, and the one thing the game never names by its number. The pressed row is the brush: a block makes Place lay that kind, and a thing makes Place put it on the face you click in place of whatever stood there, so the tip's last line names it and its icon stands on the hovered face. `1` to `9` press the shown group's rows and `j` and `k` step through every row across the groups; Alt-click takes whatever is under the pointer as the brush, the thing on the face with its colour and its facing, or the kind of the block. A teleporter, a switch, an exit, a hidden exit or moving spikes work on a plain block only, nothing stands on a crumbling or vanishing block's faces, and a face that looks into another block takes nothing: each is said and refused. Placing a thing of a kind the legend hides brings the kind back.

A moving platform or a laser is a run, laid in two clicks. While editing, the panel that opens on a selected block of the draft is also its form, standing above the block's own rows, which fold under "Block information" and stay open or folded as they were last left, from block to block. A row of six buttons, Faces, stands for the block's faces, a dot on each that holds something, the chosen one pressed and a face that looks into another block greyed and saying which; choosing one selects it, as a click on the map does. Under it the block's kind can be changed, a vanishing block's phase set, a platform's length and speed typed and a laser's colour chosen, the axis and the far end of a run shown as they are, and a run's other cells show the run's own block; what the chosen face holds is a list grouped as the palette is, with nothing at its head, and the thing on it can be faced one of its four ways, under Facing, given its colour, worth or phase, and a teleporter says where it leads. A face that holds a switch or a teleporter, and a laser's block, show the circuit of their colour in one row: its swatch, whether it starts on or off, which the button turns over, and how many switches, teleporters and lasers share the colour, the row the Level card shows for all four, read as the card's is, the button named by its colour and the swatch for the eye alone. "Place the brush here" and "Build against this face" lay what a click on that face would, so a level can be built from the keyboard; the Place button is greyed while the face holds what it would place, keeping the focus and saying so when pressed, and with a platform or a laser for the brush there is none, since a run is laid on the map in two clicks. The panel keeps to the block and its face: how the level plays, its counts and what the checks find are the level line's note, so they are read once rather than on every block. Every change is one step that undo takes back, a change the game would not forgive is refused and put back, as the tools refuse it, a Length or a Speed that is not a number is put back and says so, an emptied one, or one that rounds to what the block holds, in silence, and the field being worked keeps the focus while the map redraws.

On a touch screen there is no hover, so Place and Erase take two taps: the first tap on a face marks it as the hover would, with the wash, the ghost or the brush's icon and a line on the strip saying what a second tap does, and the second tap on the same face does it, though a first tap on a face that holds what the brush would put there says so and marks nothing; a tap elsewhere marks that face instead, and a drag or a pinch, which turn and pan and zoom as ever, marks nothing. ## How it is built

`public/` is the deploy artifact: dependency-free ES modules, no build step, but for pako's deflate under `js/vendor/`, vendored with its licence, which writes a zlib stream byte for byte as zlib does where the browser's own compressor does not, so that a level fits a slot on the disc the same in every browser. `js/main.js` boots, `js/viewer/state.js` holds shared state and the camera, and modules talk through the `emit`/`on` pair in `js/viewer/ui/dom.js` rather than importing each other both ways. The modules lie in layers, and a module imports only its own layer and those beneath it: under `js/viewer/`, what a plain visit loads, the root is the model, which touches neither the page nor a canvas, `ui/` the page's primitives, which know nothing of the map, `draw/` what paints the canvases and `chrome/` what stands beside the map and speaks for it; `js/format/` reads and writes the level, the project, the pack, the disc image, the patch and the store, touches no DOM and reaches only the model; and `js/editor/` is what loads when a level of your own is wanted. The page itself starts what the boot will ask for before any of its modules has arrived: every module the boot imports, so they load in one round rather than as each is found in the last, the data files, and the textures of the world the link's pack names, or the first world's where there is no link, so everything is on its way together. Once that world's textures have landed and the page is idle, the other worlds' are fetched one at a time behind whatever the page asks for itself, from the next world on, unless the browser is set to save data, so a change of world finds them here; `js/viewer/draw/atlas.js` keeps every atlas for the visit, and a frame redraws for a landing only when it is the shown world's. Every level reaches the screen through one funnel in `js/viewer/chrome/navigate.js`, the disc's by their place in the data and any other through `showLevel`, and a module may provide levels for a link to name beyond the disc's slots; a level from elsewhere installed again is an update, which keeps the hidden kinds, the slice and the selection where the level still has its cell. `js/viewer/draw/render.js` draws the frame: it puts the cells in the painter's order, keeps the pick canvas that turns a point on the screen into a cell, lays what shows through the blocks over the map, and draws again while anything moves. It draws the blocks through `js/viewer/draw/blocks.js`, which lays each world's textures from `js/viewer/draw/atlas.js` and the light a device casts on them from `js/viewer/draw/lights.js`, what stands on them through `js/viewer/draw/things.js`, a laser's beam through `js/viewer/draw/beams.js`, and what is neither, from a platform's route and the ground grid to the scale bar and the lettering of a label, through `js/viewer/draw/overlays.js`, which draws the arrow to where a teleporter leads with `js/viewer/draw/arrow.js`, a link drawn as OddworldMap draws its links and knowing nothing of either map. What draws over the map from outside it registers with the frame's three hooks, one run before any block where the ground grid is drawn, one after each block and one once the see-through layer is laid, and what listens to the pointer registers with `js/viewer/chrome/interaction.js` for a click, for a drag, which what registers may take from the camera at the press, and for what about the cell under the pointer matters to the hover, which is how the editor draws its grid, washes and ghost and takes its clicks and drags; and a module may fill the panel through `onShow` in `js/viewer/chrome/detail.js` and stand a note of its own under the level line through `noteWith` in `js/viewer/chrome/navigate.js`, which asks what is registered before it falls back to the level's own note, and those two seams are how the editor's form and a draft's overview come in. `js/editor/editor.js` and what it imports, pako included, load when a level of your own is first made, opened or linked to, or when this browser already holds one, after the map's first frame unless the link names one of them, which waits for the editor, and never on a plain visit, which carries the editor's markup and styles alone. Beside the frame, `js/viewer/draw/compass.js` paints the compass on a canvas of its own: a block in the camera's own projection whose faces take the shades the level's faces do, so it turns exactly as the level does. Every frame drawn asks for it, and it paints again only when the turn, the world or the textures have changed.

The camera is a plain orbit: a yaw and a pitch give three unit vectors, and every point is projected orthographically onto two of them with the third as depth. Cubes are drawn back to front by that depth, which is exact for equal cubes on a lattice, and a face is drawn only when its outward normal turns toward the camera and no block the game hides that face behind stands against it. Lighting comes from a fixed direction in the world rather than from the screen, so a face keeps its brightness as the view turns and the solid goes on reading as solid.
