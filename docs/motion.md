# How each thing moves

What the executable does to every object and every moving block each frame, read off the code of the NTSC-U release with `tools/mips.py`, and what each reading rests on. `python3 tools/kula_motion.py` prints every number below with the address it was read at and stops if an instruction is not the one it expects, and `--table` prints what the build ships under `motion` in `public/objects.json`: the rates by type and by kind of block in the game's units, and the two cycles below as a frame program per phase, simulated from their routines and cut at one frame for all four phases so the game's spacing between them survives. The viewer draws everything that moves on the spot from that table, on a clock of sixty frames a second that starts at the level's first frame as the level opens, as play's does, and what travels across blocks does so from the same table and the rules below, one game frame at a time; the Motion switch holds all of it at that first frame, except for a few things posed to show what they are. [level-format.md](level-format.md) is the same document for the level file and [ggi.md](ggi.md) for the meshes.

## The frame

**Everything below is per frame, and a frame is a sixtieth of a second.** The play loop at `0x80040210`-`0x800406c4` runs one level a pass (`0x80040690` calls `0x80040718`), and that routine loops a frame at a time from `0x80040aa0` to `0x80041c34`. Each pass waits once, at `0x80051d08`, for a flag that a callback installed through the system library before the loop sets (`0x8003fdb4`, installed at `0x80040a7c`), which every rate play has borne out puts at once a vertical blank; unless one of two words, `0x800a572c` and `0x800a592c`, is set, in which case the wait is `VSync(2)` and a counter at `0x8004188c` ticks by two instead of one, and nothing in the executable writes either. Play bears out that the level's clock ticks once a pass, 50 ticks to a second it shows: OBJ LEVEL's 99 seconds are then 4950 passes, and play counted 57 moves of a wandering ball in them, at 86 a move, where 99 seconds of sixtieths would have held 69. A cross-check comes from the level data: a moving platform's speed on the disc is 30 units a frame on all 37, and the loader at `0x80032a5c` scales it by 50/60 before use, which is what a game authored for a 50 Hz release does when it is built for 60. The rates that live in the code carry no such scaling, so on the PAL disc they may run at 50 a second or may have been retuned; this disc says nothing about that.

Angles are in 4096ths of a turn, the unit the sine table at `0x8005fb58` takes; distances are in units of which a block is 512; and one word, `0x800a33d0`, gates every update below with a test against zero. It selects a second view the shipped game never draws: it is a data word that loads as 0 and is never written, so the gates are always open.

## The things on a block

The routine at `0x80039200` walks every record of kinds 0 to 4, every slot of each, and every object whose `f4` is not zero, and both advances and draws it; the table at `0x80010290` sends each type to its own case. **An object's angles live in its own slot**: `f11`, `f12` and `f13`, which this document calls A, B and C, are set at load (`0x80035930`) to random values up to 4095 for every type the loader knows, so the phase of every pickup is random on every visit; the boost button alone starts at A = 16. Which angle turns which axis is named by the game as x, y and z, and its z is the axis [ggi.md](ggi.md) calls y, the normal of the face the thing stands on, since a coin's spin is its z. The bob some types have, and the catalogue star's circling, are an offset the routine adds to the object's position, and before it does it turns the offset by the object's own matrix (`0x80039cf0`), the one `0x80039ec0` builds from its face and facing, so a bob is along the face's normal on whatever face the thing stands, twenty units either way where a block is 512, and the circling is in the face's plane.

| type | what it does, per frame |
| --- | --- |
| 31 key, 37 coin, 38 sunglasses | turns about the normal by −75, a turn in 0.91 s; bobs by 20·sin C + 10 with C stepping −58, a cycle in 1.18 s |
| 36 gem | turns by 29, a turn in 2.35 s; no bob |
| 43 to 47 fruit | turns by 20 (3.4 s a turn); tilts by 150·sin B about the first tangent, 13° either way, with B stepping −66 (1.03 s); bobs by 20·sin C + 10 with C stepping −50 (1.37 s) |
| 35 hourglass | swings by 2048·sin A + 30 about the second tangent, half a turn either way and back, with A stepping 26, so it goes over twice in 2.63 s; and turns about the normal by −16, a turn in 4.3 s |
| 32 to 34 pills | flips about the second tangent by 66 (1.03 s a turn) while turning about the normal by 20 (3.4 s) |
| 42 catalogue star | A steps 13 and the form is the brown model while A is below 2049 and the green one after, so it changes every 2.6 s; it circles its block's centre at radius 100 with C stepping 83 in the brown form (a lap in 0.82 s) and 6 in the green (11 s), turned about the normal by 1024 − C; a third angle B grows while green and is not drawn with |
| 5 teleporter | turns about the normal by 25 (2.7 s) while its `f4` is 1, on, and stands still while it is off, so one that starts off stands at the random angle the loader gave it |
| 7 exit, 26 hidden exit | turns by −20 (3.4 s) while shut, `f4` = 2, and −45 (1.5 s) once open; the last key sets the exit's `f3` to 0, the green model, and `f4` to 1, and gives the hidden exit the `f4` the loader took from it (`0x80038a78`, `0x80035898`) |
| 9 switch, 11 moving spikes, 12 spikes, 28 arrow | no turn; the moving spikes' frame is written by the routine below into `f8`, which the draw command carries (`0x8002d920`) |
| 10 boost button | reacts to the ball and does nothing on its own: A is a height of 16, sinks by 1 a frame, to flat and no lower, while the ball is within 700 units, and rises by 4 a frame after, the model's height scaled by A/16 along its normal (`0x80039b48`): flat in 16 frames, a quarter of a second, and back in 4. Once A is back to 16 or more the game draws the model unscaled, so a rise from a height that is not a multiple of 4 overshoots by up to 3/16 for one frame and no longer |
| 39 to 41, 48, 49 | the placeholder arrows turn by −14 |

The variant a type draws is `f3` for every type, read by the draw dispatcher at `0x8002d878` as the model `type × 16 + f3` in the second table of the .GGI, which is why a coin's tier, a gem's cut and a teleporter's colour are all the same field, and why type 42 changes its shape by writing its own `f3`. Three more things happen in the draw and not in the walk: a teleporter, a switch or an exit whose `f4` is 1 turns a light on the faces round it over every 19 frames (`0x8002d424`, and "The devices' light" below); the exit's face is marked open when no keys are left (`0x8002c150`); and a fruit's type is rewritten at load to the lowest of the five whose bit in the word at `0x800a5664` is clear (`0x80035664`), which reads as the first not yet collected and is the rule the map states as the level's number counted round the five. The loader also numbers each object it knows in the order it meets them and writes that into `f5` (`0x80035998`), the index of its runtime entry, so the number the disc holds there is not the one the game plays by.

The viewer steps a boost button's count as the game does, a frame at a time, and presses it with the ball at the level's start, measured as the game measures it, from the point where the ball touches its face: the button's routine reads the ball's position (`0x80039b74`), which the start sets to its cell's centre and 256 along its face's normal (`0x800361c8` to `0x8003621c`), and the button stands where it does on its own face; the six beside a start, on LEVEL 29, 30 (two), 42 and 91 and SIMON 8, sink flat as a level opens, as they do in play. Measured that way rather than from block to block, the ball leaves alone the two buttons on SIMON 8 and LEVEL 119 that stand on a neighbour's far face, 724 units away. The block under the pointer and the selected block press a button too, each as the ball standing on that block's face the button stands on, since a block has no face of its own to give; so a button sinks as the pointer reaches its block or one beside it, springs back when the pointer leaves, and stays down while its block or a neighbour is selected.

## The ball

The ball at rest breathes. Its draw at `0x80031024` scales it by two matrices, one for the squash of a landing, which decays over a few frames, and one for an idle swell that never stops (`0x800325c0`): an angle steps by 40 a frame plus a fortieth of what the level's time is short of 6000 ticks, the time being kept in fiftieths of a second (`0x80035f90`), and the ball is drawn 200·sin of it wider across the face, in 4096ths, and twice that shorter along the normal; the landing's matrix is wider by its own amount and shorter by the same. Both scale the ball about its centre, and the draw then moves the centre along the normal by the ball's radius of 100 times what the two have taken off its height (`0x800314c4`), so its underside stays on the face and its top alone rises and falls. On a level with 99 seconds that is a breath every 62 frames, just over a second, quickening to one every 22 as the clock runs down; the squash is applied after the ball's roll and before its face's orientation, so it is always along the face the ball stands on.

## The moving spikes

Type 11 keeps a state in the kind word of the slot after its own, a countdown in `f15` (the last pad of its slot) and the frame it shows in `f8`, and the routine at `0x8002bbec` steps them, dispatching on the state through `0x8001005c`:

| state | what it is | lasts | the frame shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | down | 63 | 0 |
| 1 | rising | 6 | 0, 0, 6, 12, 19, 25 then 31; `f4` becomes 1, deadly, and sound 105 plays as it starts |
| 2 | rattling | 15 | 31 on even counts, and 2, 6, 11, 15, 20, 25, 29 on odd ones between them |
| 3 | held up | 14 | 31 |
| 4 | retracting | 45 | 31 falling to 0 in steps of 32/44; `f4` becomes 2, harmless, with 11 frames left; sound 106 as it starts |

**The cycle is 143 frames, 2.4 s, and `f3` is a phase of 35 frames a step.** The loader at `0x8002a078` sets the first state from `f3 × 35`: under 63 it starts down with 63 − 35·f3 to go, under 96 held up with 96 − 70 to go, under 140 retracting with 140 − 105 to go, so on the first cycle a phase of 2 holds the spikes up for 26 frames instead of 14 and a phase of 3 starts them three quarters up; from the second cycle on all four run the same 143 frames, 35, 72 and 107 frames apart. `f3` is set to 0 at load, so every one draws the same model, and the 32 frames of the mesh go from flat to 388 units tall in equal steps. Simulating the routine from each phase is what gives the frame lists and the 143; `python3 tools/mips.py --at 0x8002bbec --count 184` shows it.

## The vanishing block

Kind 7 keeps its state in the first slot's `f3` and a countdown in `f4`, and the routine at `0x8002b6ec` steps them through the six states of `0x80010044`; the first slot's `f2` on the disc, 0 to 3, is the phase, and the loader at `0x80028b14` turns it into a state:

| state | what it is | lasts | what shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | absent | 91 | nothing; the lattice cell is −1 |
| 1 | fading in | 9 | the cell is set and the faces brighten from black to white |
| 2 | settling | 5 | from 211 down to neutral grey |
| 3 | solid | 91 | neutral, `0x808080` |
| 4 | dimming | 9 | from 57 up to 231 with the faces' translucent flag on |
| 5 | flashing out | 19 | white fading to black; then the cell is −1 again |

**The cycle is 224 frames, 3.7 s, and `f2` is a phase of 56 frames a step**, a quarter exactly: a phase under 2 starts absent with 91 − 56·f2 to go and the others start solid with 196 − 56·f2 to go. The block is in the lattice, and can be stood on, from the moment it starts fading in to the moment it finishes flashing out, 133 frames, and out of it for 91. What the disc holds in the record's `type` word is overwritten at load with the lattice's own value for the cell, which is what the routine writes back when the block returns.

## The invisible block

Kind 3 is hidden until the ball comes near it, and the routine at `0x80051318` decides that every frame. It reads a struct at `0x800a5a58`: the point where the ball touches its face, its position with a platform's travel added, which the ball's routine writes there every frame (`0x80036914`), a near and a far distance, and a flag. While the sunglasses last (the pickup that scores 500 starts their timer at `0x8002bec8`, and `0x8002bf50` counts it down) every invisible face is drawn and nothing else happens. Otherwise every invisible face is first hidden, or shown where the flag is set, and then each invisible block near the ball is lit corner by corner: full within the near distance, falling in a straight line to nothing at the far one, and where the flag is set the other way round, nothing within the near distance and full from the far one out. A face with any corner lit is drawn, shaded between its corners' brightnesses, and added to what is behind it.

**The blocks it lights are those in a box of cells about the ball**, on each axis from the cell the far distance takes the ball to one way to the cell it takes it to the other, a point being in the cell it falls in, no lower than 0 and no higher than 33 (`0x80051418` onward), and of those only the blocks of kind 3 (`0x80051558`). Of each face it takes the four vertices the face routines laid the texture on, in their order (`0x80051594`), the texture's top left, top right, bottom left and bottom right, and measures each from the ball: the geometry coprocessor squares the three differences (`0x800515d0`), and the root comes from a table at `0x80073ab0`, a row for each count of leading zeros in the sum (`0x800515f0`), which draws a straight line between the roots of the powers of two either side, each rounded, so it falls short of the true root between them, by up to 7 units at 418 and 26 at 1695. A corner within the near distance is 128. Past it the corner is 128 less how far past it the corner is times 128 over the stretch between the two distances, in fixed point (2^30 over the stretch, the product shifted down 23), and no lower than nothing (`0x80051644` to `0x80051680`); where the flag is set it is 128 less that (`0x8005168c`). The four brightnesses are the face's corner colours (`0x800516ac`).

**A face's first byte says how it is drawn**, which the transform at `0x8005662c` reads every frame: bit 0 draws it, bit 1 blends it with what is behind, bit 2 drops it where it faces away, and bit 4 shades it between the four corner colours at bytes 16 to 28 of the face instead of drawing it flat in the colour at 12 (`0x800568d0`). The light writes 0 into every invisible face, or 7 where the flag is set or the sunglasses last, drawn flat; then 22 into each face of a block in the box, and 23 where any of its corners is lit (`0x80051698`), drawn and shaded between them. The colour at 12 is the one the animator pulses, and the animator writes the face's frame of shimmer into its top byte whatever the light does, so a lit face runs through its seven frames at the steady brightness of its corners, without the pulse. On the seven levels below, a face outside the box is drawn as it is through the sunglasses, and one in the box is steady at 128 past the far distance and fades as the ball comes near.

The loader sets the struct at `0x80027bf8`: 350 and 512 and no flag, so a block shows only within about a block of the ball; and on a level whose settings record, kind 9, carries a first field of 1 (`0x80027c78`), 1280 and 1792 with the flag, so its invisible blocks show everywhere, fade as the ball comes within three and a half blocks and are gone within two and a half. Seven levels do: LEVEL 106 to 108, 110 and 111, SIMON 9 and HIDDEN 8. The table ships both pairs of distances, the settings' kind and the value that turns the light round, full and the shift of its fixed point, and the root's rows, as kind 3's `light`.

With the sunglasses the faces are drawn flat, through the colour the animator at `0x8004f3b8` pulses from 48 to 136 of 128 over 90 frames while they cycle through their seven frames of shimmer over 144, which is the look the map draws them in, so that they can be seen at all.

The viewer lights them as the game does, from the ball at the level's start and from the block under the pointer and the one selected, each as the ball standing on that block's face that looks the way the lit face does, the stand-ins the boost button takes: a face of a block in a stand-in's box is shaded between its corners' brightnesses, the nearest stand-in's at each corner, and the sunglasses' look fills in as much as each corner falls short of full, so a face in full light is exactly the game's and one out of reach keeps that look, with nothing darker between. On the seven levels that look is the game's own for a face outside the box, and a face in it is drawn in the shading alone, as the game draws it, so it fades out as a stand-in comes near. The corners' shading is interpolated evenly across the face, which is what the game's quartered near faces come to. The light is one of the effects, which stand where the level starts while the Motion switch holds it, so there nothing is lit.

## The devices' light

**A teleporter, a switch or an exit that is on casts a coloured light on the faces round it, on for 19 frames and off for 19.** The draw of the things on a block (`0x8002d2ac`, which the walk calls for each of them at `0x80039e5c`) sends types 5, 7 and 9 through its table at `0x80010074` to a case each, and the hidden exit, 26, to none. While the thing's `f4` is 1 the case counts `f15`, the last pad of its slot, down, and when it reaches nothing sets it back to 19 and turns its light over: it makes a light from the thing's cell and face (`0x8002e3d8`) and keeps its handle in the low byte of the kind word of the slot after, with 256 over it to say it holds one, or lets the one it holds go (`0x8002d994`), leaving 255. While it holds one it gives it its colour every frame (`0x8002f2b4`), and while `f4` is not 1 it lets it go and counts nothing. The loader starts every one of the three on a plain block, which all 441 are, at 19 and holding none (`0x80029744`), and the faces take their light (`0x80051020`) before the walk that turns it over (`0x80036958`, both in the frame's draw at `0x8002a3c0`), so a thing on from the start is dark for the level's first 19 frames and lit for the next 19, lit while the frame over 19, rounded down, is odd. One turned off loses its light at once and stops counting, and once on again counts on from where it stopped, so it comes back on a phase of its own.

**A light is a list of faces with a level at each corner.** The list at `0x800df184` holds 32 lights of eight words: a colour, −1 while the light is free, then up to seven faces, each a pointer with a byte of four two-bit levels over it, 0, 1 or 2 (`0x8002f16c`). `0x8002e3d8` puts the thing's own face on it at 2 all round, then for each of the face's four edges the first of three faces that is there: the face of the block diagonally out over the edge that looks back across it, a wall rising from the edge; else the face of the block beside the edge that looks the way the thing's does; else the face of the thing's own block down from the edge. The first two take 2 at their two corners on the edge and nothing at the other two, and the third 1 at its two on the edge. A face is there where the lattice holds a block that has the face, as the loader built it, so a face built unseen behind a crumbling block counts and one the loader built nothing for does not. The levels are given in the order of the face's corners at its texture's first turn, and `0x8002f16c` turns them by the face's own turn, its byte at 6, so they stay with the block. `tools/kula_motion.py` runs `0x8002e3d8` for each face with that call answered by hand to read the faces and levels it tries, in order.

**Each frame the faces of a light are shaded between their corners with the light added.** `0x800511c0`, before the invisible block's light, marks every face of every light as shaded between its corners and gives each corner the face's flat colour; `0x80051020`, after it, adds to each corner the light's colour for level 2, seven sixteenths of it for level 1, each channel rounded down (the coprocessor multiplies the channels, sixteen times over, by 1792 of 4096, and the result comes back down by sixteen), and nothing for 0, the lowest bit of each channel cleared, and a channel that passes 255 stands at 254; and `0x80051254`, once the faces are drawn, marks them flat again. So the thing's face is lit all over, and the light fades across the next face on each side to nothing at its far edge.

**The colour is by world and circuit.** A teleporter and a switch take theirs from 40 words at `0x80073048`, four for each world in pack order, by `f3`, the circuit, where 128 is a channel's own brightness: yellow is 25 of red and green in six worlds, green 25 of green and red 25 of red in most, and a few take theirs brighter, up to 64; blue is 25 of blue in Atlantis, 32 in Arctic, 44 in Haze, 89 in Field, 128 in Hills, Inca, Cowboy and Hell, 192 in Mars and 204 in Hiro. An exit takes one word a world from `0x800a32e0`, the same green, 16, 64 and 16, in all ten. So in most worlds a blue device lights the faces round it far more than the others do. The table ships the count, level 1's share of the colour, what a light takes for each face and each device's colours by world, by its type, as `glow`.

The viewer casts the light of every teleporter and switch that is on, as the game does, turned over by the same count, which runs only while the device is on, so one switched off and on again from its panel keeps its own phase. What each light takes is found once for a level, against the lattice as the level opens, since nothing beside a device breaks, vanishes or moves; a beam's cells hold no block, as a dark beam's do in the game. At each corner the lights on a face are summed and added to the colour the face is drawn through, a channel standing at 254 once it passes that, and the face's texture, times what is added over 128, is added to what the face shows, shaded between its corners as the invisible block's light is. The map leaves the light off an invisible face, which its own light draws. An exit is shut as the level loads it and casts no light. The light is one of the effects, so while the Motion switch holds the level it stands as at the level's first frame, dark.

## The crumbling block

Kind 6's first slot holds 512 times its cell in `f2` to `f4` on the disc and the game does nothing with that: the loader at `0x80028a98` writes 512 into `f2`, and once the ball has broken the block (its `type` word goes from 1 to 2, then 3) the routine at `0x8002b3f4` takes 26 off `f2` a frame and drops the block from the lattice when it goes negative, twenty frames later, a third of a second, spawning debris from each face on the way.

## The moving platform

Kind 5's record is read three ways at once. Its `type` is the direction it is travelling, in the game's numbering of the six; `f4` to `f6` and `f7` to `f9` are the two ends of the run; the second slot is `[which end, blocks long, speed, dwell, a number, −1]`, the length 2 or 3 and the speed 30 on every one of the 37; and the extras hold its position, 512 times its cell, with three words of 256 after. The routine at `0x80032af4` moves the position by the speed along the direction each frame that the dwell is 0, dispatching on the direction through `0x80010158`, and on reaching the far end reverses the direction (1 and 4, 2 and 3, 0 and 5 swap), flips the end word and sets the dwell to 48 frames, 0.8 s; the dwell counts down and sound 107 plays the frame before it leaves. **At load the dwell is zeroed and the speed becomes 30 × 50 / 60 = 25**, 1500 units a second, a block in a third of a second (`0x8003278c`). The record's faces are drawn at that position, one block after another along `f2`'s axis, by `0x8002b26c`.

## The laser

Kind 8 is built at load by `0x800280d0` and drawn every frame by `0x80051754`, which is written by hand against the geometry coprocessor. The builder lays a beam from the first cell its record names to the second, one stretch for each empty cell between, and all 123 name the lower end first; it never reads the record's `type`, and it gives both ends the same plate ([tgi.md](tgi.md)), so neither end is the emitter. A stretch reaches 256 units either way of its cell's centre, face to face, and carries four lines 90 units off the axis on both axes across it. Each line is two flat quads 14 units wide, crossed along it, and a one-pixel line down their middle. The renderer files a cell's stretches by the depth of the cell's centre and draws them in the blend that adds them to what is behind, the draw mode `0xe1000020`; a dark beam, `f3` 0, it skips.

**A line's colour is its circuit's channels times a level it steps through on its own.** The builder makes a table per circuit (`0x80028128`) out of 240 levels at `0x8009a040`, each from 0 to 31, shifted into red and green for circuit 0, blue for 1, green for 2 and red for 3, so a full level is 248 in each channel it lights. Each of a beam's four lines starts at a random place in the table (`0x800284e0`) and steps one entry a frame, so the table runs through in 4 s and the four lines shimmer out of step: it holds 31 on most frames, with a few fades, dips as low as 4 and runs of 31 and 12 on alternate frames. The map hashes each line's start from its beam's first cell, and draws nothing for a dark beam, as the game does, but a broken line among the outlines.

**The lattice follows the switch.** Every frame, after drawing, the renderer writes −1 into every cell a dark beam crosses (`0x80051c0c`) and then −2 into every cell a lit one crosses (`0x80051c64`), so a dark beam's cells are open to the ball and to a captivator's probe alike, and where two beams cross, as on LEVEL 96, 98, 105 and 124 and FINAL 10, a lit one fills the cell. A switch turns the beam over in its record ([level-format.md](level-format.md)), so the writes follow it from the next frame on.

## The captivators

Types 50, 51, 52, 53 and 56 are lifted out of their slots at load into 180-byte entries at `0x800ba3dc` (`0x8003b5dc`), each standing **456 units from its block's centre along the face normal**, 200 off the face, with the normal, a home position, a heading from `f2` and three angles of its own. `0x8003ba88` moves them each frame and `0x8003d680` turns them, and the `f3` of a corkscrew alone survives as a phase. **Each is drawn with its model's origin where its entry is**: the draw copies the entry's position and turns it into the view as it is (`0x8003d81c`, `0x8003ed3c`, `0x8003f3e8`, `0x8003dea0`), so the stars, the wheel and the wandering ball all have their middles 200 off the face whatever their size, which leaves the small wandering ball clear of the floor by more than half its height; the corkscrew alone is drawn 150 nearer its face than its entry (`0x8003e5d8`), 50 off, and bounces from there. The table ships that height with each of the five as its `entry`, and the viewer stands them there rather than on their lowest points.

**Where a star or the wheel goes is decided against the lattice, with no dice.** An entry keeps a heading and a side, the two tangents of its face turned by its `facing` (`0x8003f98c`, which lays the face's axes and turns them for a facing of 2, 3 or 4 alone: a facing of 1 heads along the second tangent, each further value a quarter turn on, the side following, and any value it does not turn for heads as 1 does, the 0 that 28 of the 46 wandering balls carry among them), and asks the lattice about the block beside it on either side and the block ahead through `0x8003d278`, `0x8003d3f4` and `0x8003cf6c`: a probe looks 400 units back toward the block, into its cell, and one block along the side or the heading, and a block counts if `0x8003fc00` says it can be stood over, which is any plain style, a crumbling block, or a record whose slot for that face is empty, holds a captivator, the start, fire, ice or type 4, and never a moving platform, a coin, a key or a device; going ahead also needs the cell above the block ahead empty, and a lit beam's cells count as filled, a dark beam's and a vanishing block's while it is gone as empty. Turning is `0x8003d378`, `0x8003d4f4` and `0x8003d22c`: toward the side, toward the other side, or about, and a turn swaps the heading and the side and touches nothing else; what is drawn is RotMatrix of the entry's own three angles (`0x8003d7c8`), so a star tumbles on unbroken through a turn, and the wheel's nose swings round only because its turn steps one of its angles.

The slow star decides each time it crosses the middle of a cell along its heading, which `0x8003fca4` measures: if there is a block beside it on the side it turns onto it, else if there is one on the other side it turns that way, else if it cannot go on it turns about; and it walks 13 a frame. The wheel rolls while it can go on, and only when it cannot, at the next middle of a cell, does it choose, the other side first, then the side, then about; it stands for 77 frames while its nose swings a quarter or half round, 13 or 26 a frame and square on the last, and rolls on at 12 a frame with its roll at −53 only while moving; the viewer swings the nose evenly all the way round instead, without the game's click of 3° or 6° at the end. The wandering ball settles on the grid after each block, draws one of the four ways until it finds one open, 0 the side, 1 the other side, 2 on and 3 back (`0x8003c730`), then shakes toward it for 76 frames and dashes; the viewer draws its ways from the game's dice, as below, and steps every walker of a level together, in the game's order, a frame at a time.

**The wandering ball's dice are seeded once, as its level starts.** They are the game's one random routine (`0x80047418`): a word of state multiplied by 1103515245 and added 12345 at each draw, a draw of n being n times the state's low half over 65536. The play loop runs a level through one routine (`0x80040690` calls `0x80040718`), which seeds that state with 1 before anything else (`0x80040748` calls `0x80025054`, whose first act is to call `0x8004740c` with 1) and then loops a frame at a time from `0x80040aa0` to `0x80041c34`, so a restart seeds it afresh and nothing else does. In the loop the balls draw in the order the loader lifted them, record by record and face by face (`0x8003b5dc`), each until it finds a way open, and the sequence runs on from one decision to the next. Three other things draw from it, none while the ball stands still: the screen's shake (`0x80022814`), only while the screen shakes; the effects (`0x80011ac0`), only while one runs, and one is started by the ball's contacts (`0x800118c4`, called from `0x80038548` onward); and the pause and game-over menus. So a level watched without playing walks its balls by their own draws alone, 1, 2, 3, 2, 3, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 0 and on from the start, the same on every play, and play on OBJ LEVEL bore that out move for move, 58 moves of the top ball from the start until the time ran out; a pickup collected, a shake or a pause on the way can shift the sequence for everything after, as far as the three routines' gates show.

The moving platform's run is in [level-format.md](level-format.md): from its cell toward the far end at 25 a frame, stopping when it reaches or passes the end, 48 frames still, then back, and its blocks are laid from its cell along the positive way of its axis.

## The shadows

Under a pickup the loader paints the shadow into the face ([tgi.md](tgi.md)). Under the ball and the five captivators the game draws one every frame, **a flat square 300 units across laid on the face, textured with one of two sprites and taken away from what is behind it.** Nothing else has one: the square is drawn by the ball's draw (`0x80031024`, through `0x8002ca2c`) and the captivators' (`0x8003d680`, through `0x8002c4b0`), and its two sprites are read by nothing but the loop that sets up its primitives.

**The square.** Both routines transform the same four vertices, at `0x800a32c0`, 150 either way in x and z, by a matrix their caller builds, and drop the square when its corners wind the wrong way on the screen (`0x80051e8c`), so a shadow shows only on a face turned toward the view. The caller builds the matrix with `0x8003a114` from the face and a point it moves toward the block by a distance it gives, and turns it a quarter about x before the rest (1024, `0x8003db78`), which lays the square flat on the face.

**The sprites and the blend.** `0x80029048` sets up the primitives once, sixteen of each of two kinds: a textured quad, semi-transparent, drawing the sprite 14 or 15 past the group the .GGI header's sixth bound starts, which the parser keeps at `0x800a5738` (116, `0x80022f28`), so [ggi.md](ggi.md)'s sprites 130 and 131, both 32 by 32 at 4 bits: a soft round blot and a square with rounded corners. Both uploads ask for blend 2, which the sprite table puts in the page word (`0x80023198`) and which takes each texel away from what is behind, and their palettes run from 0, drawn as nothing, up to 10 of 31 in each channel, so a shadow's middle takes 10 of 31 off whatever it lies on, darkening a pale stone a little and a dark one to black, and falls off evenly to nothing at its rim, 150 units out. The quad's colour, written into it with each draw (`0x8002c990`), scales every texel by itself over 128, and is how strong the shadow is.

## Not settled

Which of the two tangents a pill flips about and a fruit tilts about rests on the naming of the model's axes in [ggi.md](ggi.md) and on RotMatrix's order; a pill in play flips the way the map does. What the PAL release runs at is not on this disc. The laser's beam was not looked at for a pulse. In one state of the ball, where the word at 356 in its state is 96, h is scaled by a table before the shadow's strength is taken from it (`0x8003237c`), and that state was not traced.

Where a device's light looks for a face in a cell a lit beam crosses, the lattice holds the beam's −2, which `0x8002f16c` takes for a block and looks up 32 words before the table of faces, in memory the executable does not fill; whether it finds a face there, and so whether the light stops at that edge or falls through to the next face, is not settled. Past a dark beam's −1 it falls through, and the map lets it fall through past a lit one too.
