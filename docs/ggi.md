# The .GGI file: the objects' geometry

`/HIRO/HIRO.GGI` is the one file of its kind on the disc, 310,376 bytes, and it holds the meshes of everything the game draws in 3D that is not a block: the ball, every object a level places, and the things that move, each in its own colours, and after them the sprites and the lettering the game draws flat. It is loaded once at startup rather than per world, which is why the objects look the same in every world while the blocks change. `tools/kula_ggi.py` reads all of it.

`tools/kula_build.py` writes what the viewer draws to `public/objects.json`: for every type some level places and that has a mesh, its models at full detail, one per variant, as vertices per frame, polygons, a colour per corner and the flags, with `sorted` on a model the game draws by depth and `blend` on one with translucent polygons, in the game's units with a block 512 across. The types that move are keyed by their type numbers from the first table, on the reading below, and the fourteen balls come after, for the start.

```bash
python3 tools/kula_ggi.py --sections
python3 tools/kula_ggi.py --tables          # both model tables, and every model's shape
python3 tools/kula_ggi.py --sheet           # out/ggi/models.png, every model in its colours
python3 tools/kula_ggi.py --textures        # out/ggi/textures.png, every sprite and glyph
```

## How it was found

The filename is built at `0x8004ccf8` from a world index, an extension index into the table of five at `0x80073504` (`.TGI`, `.SFX`, `.PAK`, `.GGI`, `FI.PAK`) and a destination. `0x80040144` calls it with extension 3 and destination `0x80106000`, the buffer the `.SFX` has just been read from and consumed, and `0x80040154` hands that address to `0x80022de0`, which is the parser. The level packs and the artwork are loaded later and per world; this one is not.

## Settled

**A 13-word header, then seven sections laid end to end from byte 52.** Words 6 to 12 are the sections' sizes in `u16`, and the seventh boundary lands on the file's end exactly: S0 191,496 bytes, S1 1,920, S2 8,192, S3 8,192, S4 12, S5 204, S6 100,308. Words 13 and 14 (8 and 408) place two tables inside S0, at bytes 60 and 460. Words 0 to 5 (10, 7, 10, 44, 32, 13) are summed cumulatively by the parser into 10, 17, 27, 71, 103 and 116 and kept; what they bound is not read. Words 15 to 17 repeat the second table's length, 3,600.

**Two model tables, each of `u32` offsets from its own start**, `-1` for absent. The first has 25 entries of four words; the second 50 models of four rows of four words, and the routine at `0x80023258` indexes it as `[model][row][slot]` with strides 64, 16 and 4. That the offsets are relative to the table they sit in, not to the section, is what made the first table's models readable: relative to the section they landed in the middle of the ball.

**In the second table the model index is the object type.** Type 5 holds the teleporter, 7 the exit, 9 the switch, 10 the boost button, 11 the moving spikes, 12 the spikes, 26 the hidden exit, 28 the arrow, 31 the key, 32 and 33 the two pills, 35 the hourglass, 36 the gem, 37 the coin, 38 the sunglasses and 43 to 47 the five fruit, apple, watermelon, pumpkin, bananas and strawberry, in the order they come round in play. A row is a level of detail, three of them where the model has any and the fourth row unused, and a slot is the variant the type's varying field picks: four teleporters and four switches for the four circuit colours, three gems that are three different cuts, three coins, two exits for red and green. Types 34 and 42, which only the object catalogue places, are a third pill, a flat tablet, and two many-pointed stars. Types with no model of their own, 1, 2, 8, 29 and 30, are the ones drawn on the block's face or not at all: fire, ice, the clock, the start, which is where the ball is, and for which the viewer draws the world's ball. Types 39 to 41 and 48 to 49 all point at the arrow, which reads as a placeholder. The draw of a thing on a block reaches its model through the table as it stands (`0x8002d878`): word `type × 16 + row × 4 + f3`, the row 2 where the thing's depth passes the farther of the two distances at 292 and 296 of the struct `0x800a5764` points at, 1 where it passes the nearer and 0 otherwise, so a far thing draws a coarser model. The fruit a level shows is not its type's: the loader rewrites it to the first of the five not yet collected ([motion.md](motion.md)), so LEVEL 1's fruit is type 46, the bananas, where play shows an apple, and on the arcade levels each numbered level shows the next of the five round, which is how the map draws them.

**The first table holds fourteen balls and the things that move.** Entries 0 to 13 are spheres of radius 100, most of 98 vertices and one of 140, each entry naming the same model three times over, and the game picks among them at `0x80035ff4`: the world's place for an arcade level, so the ball is thematic, world 0, Hiro, white with coloured panels and world 3, Arctic, white and red; for a bonus level one of entries 10 to 12, by a counter the start routine steps at every level start since the game was switched on (`0x8003601c`), restarts included, so which one a player meets depends on the path they took; for a hidden level, which the code numbers mode 2, entry 13, a glass shell of 180 translucent polygons with three shards inside, green, yellow and red, drawn from the same mesh; and in the Japanese release whatever the ball designer chose. The viewer takes the world's, the glass one for a level the hidden flag marks, and for a level with no key one of the three, in the order of the bonus slots where the level stands in one and the first elsewhere; entries 20 to 24 are a three-pointed star, the wheel at three levels of detail, a four-pointed star, the hexagonal ball and the corkscrew at three levels: types 50, 51, 52, 53 and 56, the thinner star with three points being type 50 and the fuller one with four type 52, which play settled. The parser runs `0x80023258` at load over model 36's three rows and three slots, the gem's three cuts at each level of detail, and builds a 348-byte record from each of the nine, which is the only call of that routine and is what makes the gems glint ([motion.md](motion.md)); the ball's draw and the captivators' reach their models through the first table by the same two distances (`0x8002cd78`, `0x8002c7d8`).

**A model** is a header and three or four blocks:

- `i16 x, z, y` of the model's centre, `u16` bounding radius, an `i16` whose two low bits are the blend its translucent polygons take (-1, 0 or 3), `u16` flags that say how its polygons are drawn (0, 1 or 2, below), then `u32` block offsets, the first of which is 24 or 28 and so says how many there are.
- Polygons: four `u8` vertex indices each, as many as the texture block has records.
- Vertices: `u32 frames, u32 bytes per frame`, then per frame three vertices to a 20-byte group, `x1 z1 x2 z2 x3 z3 y1 y2 y3 pad` as `i16`, unused slots holding `-1`. Y is up. Every model has one frame except the moving spikes, which have 32.
- Colours: `u32 1, u32 bytes`, then 16 bytes per polygon, four corners of `r, g, b, flags`, the flags on the first corner only: `0x20` always, `0x10` for a Gouraud polygon, `0x08` for a quad, `0x02` for a translucent one. The byte is the command the GPU draws the polygon by, which the renderer hands on as it stands, and its bit 0, which would take a texture raw, is set on no polygon; the lowest bit of the first corner's red says whether the polygon is culled where it faces away (below). That these are colours and not texture coordinates is what the coins say: tier 0 is `(201, 157, 78)`, gold, tier 1 `(79, 157, 201)`, blue, tier 2 `(173, 86, 0)`, bronze; the four teleporters' panels are `(255, 255, 0)`, `(0, 0, 255)`, `(0, 255, 0)` and `(255, 0, 0)`; the exit's two variants are green and red, the lethargy pill red and yellow and the bouncy pill purple and pink, as they look in play. The shading is baked in, a teleporter's grey body running from 38 to 167 by face. The quad flag is what settled the triangles, which the fourth index could not: it is 0 on the spikes and 116 to 120 on the ball's pole, and read as quads the polygons without the flag were off their plane by a triangle's width on 88% of them, the ones with it on none. 3,306 of the 6,449 polygons are triangles.
- Normals, where there is a fourth block and bit 1 of the flags is set: packed like the vertices, unit length 4096. Only the balls have them.

**How the game draws a model.** The walk queues a command for each thing it draws (`0x8002d2ac`), and after the gem's glints `0x800530b0` turns the commands into the GPU's primitives (`0x8002aa68`): for each model a draw mode whose blend is the `i16`'s two low bits, with dithering off (`0x800533d8`), the polygons linked one after another behind it, and a draw mode that sets blend 0 with dithering on (`0x80053acc`, `0x80053b28`), so a model's own polygons are drawn undithered. The flags say in what order the polygons go (`0x8005343c`): at 0 in the order the model holds them, at 1 each by the mean depth of its corners into a table of 64 steps of 16 units about the model's own depth, which the chain runs through from the furthest step to the nearest and, within a step, from the polygon linked last to the one linked first (`0x80053b04`, `avsz3` and `avsz4`, `0x80053da8`), and at 2 in the model's order again with each corner lit by its vertex's normal (`ncct`, `0x8005446c`); 78 of the 105 models are drawn in their order, 13 by depth and the 14 balls by their normals. A polygon whose first corner's red is odd is dropped where its first three corners turn the other way on the screen (`0x80053530`, then `nclip`), which on every model is where it faces away: 5,812 of the 5,849 opaque polygons are culled so, and of the 600 translucent ones only the glass ball's 180, so every other translucent polygon is seen from both sides. A flat polygon is drawn in its first corner's colour and a Gouraud one in each corner's (`0x80053604`), every colour scaled by the light of the face its thing stands on and faded with its depth (`dcpl`). A translucent polygon meets what is behind it in its model's blend: half of each, 0, on the gem, the hourglass's glass and the glass ball's shell, and what is behind with a quarter of the polygon added, 3, on the exit's cross and on the hidden exit, which is translucent all over and so shows as a faint green glow. The viewer draws a model as the game does, in its order or by depth as its flags say, dropping a polygon the game culls where it faces away and blending a translucent one as its model says, half and half or a quarter added; it sorts by the mean depth itself rather than in the game's 64 steps, and leaves out the fade with depth and the balls' lighting by their normals.

**How the game lights a model.** The draw puts three words of light in each command (`0x8002d3c4` to `0x8002d3d0`), and the renderer multiplies every colour by them, 4096 a whole channel and 255 where a channel passes it (`dcpl`). A thing on a block takes the .TGI header's triple for the shade of its face, the same table and the same three triples its world's textures are shaded with ([tgi.md](tgi.md)), the face picking its shade by the header's word at 300 (`0x8002d3a8`) and the shade its triple from the set at byte 24, or from the set at 156, 4096 throughout, on a level the game plays as a bonus level (`0x8002d364`); so in Hiro a thing on a top shows 0.9, 0.8 and 0.8 of its colours, on the face the world lights most 1.2, 1.1 and 1.1, and on the one it lights least 0.7, 0.6 and 0.6. While a teleporter's light is lit, its light is taken again times its circuit's triple at `0x800a3308`, 1.5 in the channels its colour lights and 1.25 in the others, and a switch's or an open exit's twice over (`0x8002d49c`, `0x8002d694`, `0x8002d5c8`), so a lit device's model flashes with its light. The viewer lights what stands on a block and a lit device's model so, as the game does. The build ships each world's two sets under `skins` as `light`, and a lit device's lift with the devices' light as `lift` ([motion.md](motion.md)). The captivators take 4096 (`0x8003dd0c`), and the ball three words of its own, 4096 in each channel until something it touches tints it (`0x80032158` onward). The ball, drawn by its normals, lights each corner from a light fixed to the camera, to the left, above and in front of it in the measures 1, 4 and 2 (`0x800315e8` to `0x80031658`, from the rows of the camera's matrix that `0x80034a50` copies into the ball's state, normalised by `0x80060218`), carried into the ball's frame by its rotation (`0x80032004`): the coprocessor takes 4000 of 4096 of how squarely a normal faces the light over a background of 2048, times a base of 178 (`0x8003240c` to `0x800324a0`, `ncct`), which the renderer keeps for each vertex as a share of 256 (`0x8005443c` to `0x800544cc`); for each corner `gpf` scales the three light words by the share (`0x8005467c`) and `dcpl` applies them to the corner's colour, so a corner is drawn at between 89 and 255 of 256 of its colour, 89 where it faces away from the light and all of it from where it faces the light nearly squarely.

**No mesh is textured.** Every polygon is coloured, flat or Gouraud, and nothing in a model names a texture. The fourteen balls are the beach-ball designs the Japanese release lets a player pick from.

**The last section is the sprites and the lettering**, 148 uploads walked by `0x80022fd8` into a table of 180 twelve-byte descriptors (bpp, whether it has a palette, the GPU's clut and tpage words, the offset within the page, width, height) that the HUD code indexes. Each is `i16 bpp, i16 abr`, for a paletted one `u16 x, y` of its palette in VRAM, `i16 inline, u16 late` and the palette's words where `inline` is 0, then `u16 x, y, w, h` of the image and its pixels where `late` is 0, `w` in pixels and the data padded to four bytes; every one is a VRAM upload like the artwork's, to the right of the world's textures at `x` 496 and up. Entries 0 to 9 are the five fruit icons grey and lit, in the same order as types 43 to 47; 10 to 16 are seven 64x64 pictures (a purple glow, the ball's stripes, a red disc, an electric blue ball, a shell, two rings of sparks) that read as the sprites drawn around the teleporters and exits; 23 to 26 and 126 are the controller's four symbols; 27 to 70 and 132 to 147 the menus' words; 71 to 102 thirty-two 4x3 swatches; 119 and 125 the digits; 123 and 124 the key icon grey and gold; and 130 and 131 the two shadows the game draws under the ball and the captivators, a round one and a square one, both taken away from what is behind them ([motion.md](motion.md)). The header's six bounds (10, 17, 27, 71, 103, 116) are where these groups begin.

**The scale.** A block is 512 units: a crumbling block's record keeps its cell times 512, and the spikes and the teleporter, which cover a block's face, are 462 and 486 across. The ball is radius 100, the exit 266 wide, a coin 186. The sheet draws every model at one scale on that reading, and the viewer draws each object as itself at the same scale, standing on its face with its lowest point just off the block, a captivator at the height the game draws its entry ([motion.md](motion.md)), turned the way its record says where it has a direction, and the start as the world's ball turned the way it faces. Fire, ice and the clock, having no mesh, are the faces the game paints them as ([tgi.md](tgi.md)).

## Not settled

**Sections 1 to 5**, 1,920, 8,192, 8,192, 12 and 204 bytes; the two of 8 KB are the size of sixteen 256-colour palettes and are not needed to draw anything above.

**Which way a thing is turned**, which is read off LEVEL 62 and LEVEL 106: its `facing` field is quarter turns about the face's inward normal from the world's `+y` laid onto the face, or the world's up where the face runs across `y`, so on a block's top the arrow's tip, the model's `+z`, goes +y, -x, -y, +x for 1 to 4, and on the sides what play shows; the map turns every model the same way, the wheel's axle included.
