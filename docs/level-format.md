# The level format

What a level file holds, reading by reading, with what each reading rests on. Every figure here is computed from `public/map_data.json` or from the disc, and the readings that were once got wrong say what corrected them. [tgi.md](tgi.md) is the same document for the artwork.

## The lattice

The lattice is **fixed at 34 x 34 x 34**, and the index is `x*1156 + y*34 + z`. This was not guessed: the executable's own level walk at `0x00033f6c`-`0x00034054` is three nested loops each bounded by `slti ..., 34`, stepping the inner pointer by 1, the middle by 34 and the outer by 1156, and reading `lh` against `-1`. `tools/mips.py` will show it. `z` is the vertical axis, which the extents confirm: it is the thinnest of the three on 160 of the 230 levels. **It counts downward**, so the smallest `z` in a level is its highest block and the viewer projects screen up against it. That came from play: `OBJ LEVEL` puts one block at `z=14` against a floor of 114 at `z=17`, and in the game that block floats above the floor. A level that is one flat plane cannot show this, which is why it went unnoticed while only the first two were being checked. **Seen from above with `x` to the right, `y` counts down the page.** That is the handedness, and no cross-check on the disc can fix it, because a mirror image reads as cleanly as the original; what fixes it is lettering. `HIDDEN 10` spells VERY WELL DONE across its floor, and it reads that way round and no other, which is also the way LEVEL 1 and LEVEL 2 come out when drawn from play.

Level records vary in length only because the record table does, and several plausible-looking size relations fit a handful of levels and then fall apart; the lattice was not read off them. The check that holds is the one the build asserts: a record repeats the coordinates of the cell that points at it, on all 5540. It caught one wrong reading, that every non-zero cell names a record, when in fact the five values below 5 are block styles carrying none. It cannot catch the two below: the framing leaves the cells in the same order either way, and the check never looks inside a record.

## A record ends with its cell

The six bytes after the lattice are the level header: a signed 32-bit word that equals the placed-cell count on 79 of the 230 levels, is negative on 16, down to -51, and is unexplained on the rest, and then a `u16` record count. It ships as `header`, unnamed. Its high half is `-1` on exactly the 16 levels where the word is negative and `0` on the other 214, which is what a sign extension looks like and not what a separate flag would, and no level's count reaches the high half. Nothing in the viewer presents it as a count; what the viewer shows as a level's blocks is `placed`, derived by counting the lattice.

Then come `count + 1` stretches of 256 bytes. Read each as *a kind word, six 32-byte slots, 32 bytes never set, 26 of extras, and then the three words of the cell the record stands on*: **the cell closes a record, it does not open the next one.** The first stretch's cell words are where the header sits, so the first record's slots are not padding. The last is kind 666 and where its cell would be the disc writes three `0xFFFF`, on all 230 levels: it stands on no cell. It carries a cell for the camera to look at, two angles and the level's time, and ships as `camera`.

Framing the stretches the other way round, cell first, reads cleanly and pairs every record with the cell one slot behind it. `Level.verify` cannot tell the two apart, because the cells come out in the same order either way; that check is necessary, and it is what caught the block-style reading, but it is not sufficient. What settled the framing was play: under the wrong one LEVEL 2's key read as its first coin, LEVEL 1's two coins sat on the same rail when the game has them on opposite rails, and LEVEL 109's first laser read as its second. Under the right one every cell of both levels matches what the game shows.

## A record is a block with six faces

The six slots are the six faces of the block, in the order the game numbers directions: **0 is the top (`-z`), then `+x`, `+y`, `-y`, `-x`, and 5 the underside (`+z`).** A slot is a kind word, a type, eleven fields, a pad, a value and a pad, and a slot whose type is not zero is an object standing on that face. So a block carries up to six objects, 690 carry two, and a block whose top slot is bare is not empty: 1262 of the 1266 records that were once read as a "blue coin" on the top are a bare top with the object on another face. The kind word is the block's and is set in the first slot only, which is why a face's own kind word reads `-1` and why the same coin turns up under kinds 0, 1, 2 and 3: those are kinds of block, not of object, and the type is what names the thing.

Three things fix the face order, none of them a guess. LEVEL 1 and LEVEL 2 put everything in slot 0 and everything on top. `HIDDEN 10` puts all eighteen of its objects in slot 3, and in play the ball rolls on the `-y` face of its frame: the lettering below is a wall ahead, VERY is up and it reads left to right, which no other face gives. And a laser's `type` is the direction from its own block to the far end of the beam, on all 123 and on the 37 kind-5 records with the same layout, which is where the numbering of the six comes from. A fourth check runs on the disc alone: an object on a face cannot sit inside the neighbouring block, and under this order 8 of the 5885 objects on blocks of the four plain kinds do, against 94 for the best of the other 719 permutations. `tools/kula_faces.py` reports what every slot in the game holds.

Four kinds do not carry objects in their first two slots. A laser (8) or a rail (5) names its two ends in the first and keeps its circuit in the second, kind 6 keeps a fixed-point copy of its own cell in the first, three words that are 512 times its coordinates on all 435, which is what a block that moves would keep, though what a crumbling block does with it is not read, and kind 7 is undecoded; their other four slots are faces like any block's. Kind 9 is the last record on 160 levels and the one kind no lattice cell ever names, so the engine's walk does not reach it; its slots hold objects, and the map leaves them out and says so.

A slot has no position of its own; it is a face of the block the record stands on. 51 records are zeroed past the first slot, which puts a lattice-shaped `(0, 0, 0)` in every slot of them, and a rule that admitted any position in `0..33` as a placed entity once read 255 phantom objects out of exactly that, 51 in each of five slots, an even spread that was taken for a symmetry confirming the rule.

## Blocks and the things on them

**A lattice value below `firstRecord` and a record's kind word are the same enumeration**: 0 is a plain block, 1 fire, 2 ice, 3 invisible, and a block with something standing on it keeps its kind in the record instead of the cell. The five styles are models 7 to 11 of the shared table in the artwork file, which is where `tools/kula_tex.py` gets their skins, and a record-carrying block wears the skin of its kind. What the kinds are came from Syonyx's Roll Away walkthrough (GameFAQs, 2006, which the repo does not carry: `tools/kula_score.py --guide` takes it as a file): the level it introduces fire on is style 1 and kind 1 and nothing else new, the one it introduces ice on is 29 style-2 blocks, the one it introduces invisible platforms on is eight kind-3 records, and crumbling, moving and vanishing blocks land on the first levels with kinds 6, 5 and 7. Style 4 is one cell in each copy of `OBJ LEVEL` and nowhere else.

What stands on a face is named the same way, and then checked by arithmetic. The walkthrough states a maximum score for 209 levels, and with coins at 250, 500 and 750 by the value of their one varying field, gems 2975, keys 1000, fruit 2500, an hourglass 1190, sunglasses 500 and a crumbling block 50, the disc reproduces it exactly on 191 and holds more on the other 18, which is the way an unreachable coin or block leaves a level and what the walkthrough says of most of them; it never scores higher than the disc holds. A bonus level scores 50 for every block rolled over, and an invisible block is not one of them: the six bonus levels that have any each fall short of the walkthrough's figure by exactly 50 times their count, BONUS 10's 81 for 4050, and no other bonus level has one. `tools/kula_score.py --guide` is that check. The points live in `annotations.json`, because the disc does not hold them, and so does the walkthrough's maximum for each of the 18 levels the disc holds more on, as the level's `score` with a note naming what is out of reach: a crumbling block that cannot be broken, a coin inside a ring, the gems on a ring that cannot be left, or a pickup that shares the number in the fourth field of its slot with another and goes when that one is taken. The check then holds a level without a curated score to the walkthrough exactly, and one with it to the walkthrough and below the disc, so a curated number can neither drift from its source nor outlive its reason. The names that rest on play alone are the coin tiers' colours and which of two directional captivator types is which; the rest rests on the walkthrough's first mention landing on the game's first placement, which is the reasoning each note carries.

A level is wider than its lattice. A `kind 8` record spans a beam between two cells it names in its own fields: on all 123 in the game the pair differs on exactly one axis, the type is the direction to the far end, one field gives the axis and another says whether the beam starts lit, and no block ever stands between the two ends. The beam's colour is a word of the record's second slot, a circuit number the switches and teleporters of that colour share, 0 yellow, 1 blue, 2 green, 3 red; LEVEL 109 lines five of them up as red, yellow, green, yellow, red and the word reads `3, 0, 2, 0, 3`, and LEVEL 98's three lasers and three switches read the same three numbers. **64 of the 246 ends are cells the lattice leaves empty**, and the game stands a block on each anyway: the routine at `0x80035a70` walks the records at load, and for each kind 8 writes style 0 into the lattice at both ends and `-2` into every cell between, a value no level file holds. `OBJ LEVEL` shows it in play, carrying one lattice block above its floor where the game draws two, joined by a beam. So `placed` counts the lattice and the lattice alone, and the viewer adds the ends a beam names. A moving platform (kind 5) has the same layout and its far end is the other end of its run. Which end of a beam holds the emitter is not decoded.

## The fields of an object

The eleven fields of a slot ship as `f5` to `f15` and the value as `v`, numbered after their position in the slot, and what each means is read per type: `annotations.json` names the field that tells a type's variants apart (`by`), the one that holds the way it points (`facing`) and the one that says whether a switched device starts on (`state`). A `facing` field is not one of the six direction numbers: it takes 1 to 4 and turns the thing a quarter turn at a time on its face, which LEVEL 62's arrows fixed in play, one arrow of each value, pointing +y, -x, -y and +x on a block's top. The field after a moving thing's colour slot, 0 to 3 on the moving spikes and the corkscrew, is where in its cycle it starts, which LEVEL 122's corkscrews going up in sequence says. The two stars face along a straight run of cells a ball could stand on, to the edge of the platform or a wall, and on LEVEL 141 the four the walkthrough calls a group travelling in a line face `+x` along one seven-cell stretch with their reaches adding to the same length, which is where they patrol; the viewer does not move them yet, since in play a star turns back at any object in its way, a coin included, and that is not in the run. Two fields are the same for every type that has them.

**`f8` numbers a level's pickups.** It is set on exactly the pickup types, key, the two pills, hourglass, gem, coin, sunglasses, the five fruit and the two catalogue-only types 34 and 42, and on nothing else, and within a level it runs 0 to n-1 with no repeats on 224 of the 228 levels that have any. The four exceptions are LEVEL 134, LECEL 94, FINAL 11 and FINAL 13, and on each the walkthrough's maximum falls short of the disc by exactly the value of one object of every pair that shares a number: on 134 a lethargy pill and one of the level's two fruit share 1 and the fruit is what is missing, on 94 the hourglass and two gold coins share with the fruit and coins across the level and those three are missing, on 161 a blue gem shares 2 with a gold coin and the gem is missing, and on 163 three keys share with a gem and a coin and the keys are missing. So the game keeps what has been collected by this number, and taking one of a pair takes the other. Which one goes is not settled: it is not the earlier record on all four nor the later, and the walkthrough's routes read as the one taken first surviving, which an emulator would show at once on FINAL 13, whose HUD asks for six keys or three. The 30 pickups whose number is unset, all on BONUS 30 with their `f12`, `f13` and `v` unset too, still score, so the game numbers an unnumbered one at load; TCRF records BONUS 30 as the one arcade level rebuilt between the PAL and the NTSC release.

The record table is not a skin table: it holds coins, keys and beams, and its last entry stands on no cell at all. So every cell draws the skin of its kind and what stands on it is a marker, off the face it stands on.

**`f7` on a teleporter or a switch is its circuit's starting state**, 1 for on and 2 for off, and it is the same bit the laser record keeps for whether its beam starts lit. On every level that has lasers and switches or teleporters of one colour, the two agree, no exceptions across LEVEL 97, 98, 101, 103, 104, 105, 109, 136 and FINAL 3, 6, 10, 18 and 20, and the walkthrough reads the same way wherever it says: the teleporters it uses on arrival (LEVEL 66 to 71, BONUS 14, 125) all read 1, the ones it sends you to a button for first (84, 97, 99, 100, 101, 102, 117, 128, 133, 137, 142, 145, FINAL 7, 9, 11, 15, 18) all read 2, and LEVEL 105, all 1, is where it turns every laser off but blue and finds the green teleporter dead and the blue one live. Two of its sentences disagree, "turn off the yellow lasers" on LEVEL 103 and "deactivate the blue lasers" on LEVEL 104, where the disc has both circuits off; whether that is loose wording or the disc read wrong is one look in an emulator, and the map draws the disc's reading, a device that starts off hollow and a beam that starts dark broken.

## Level numbers and world names

The game numbers its levels straight through the worlds (`LEVEL 1`-`LEVEL 150`) and keeps the bonus, hidden, final, Simon and lesson levels in the same packs under their own names. Those names are in the level records and the game never shows them for a numbered level: the pause screen's number comes from the routine at `0x800478dc`, which computes the world's place times fifteen plus the slot plus one, and times two plus 151 for The Final. So Atlantis's `LECEL 94` plays as LEVEL 94, and Hell, whose records say 135 to 138 and then skip to 140, plays as 136 to 150 like the walkthrough counts it. The build ships that number as `shown` wherever the engine computes one.

World ids are the disc's directory names (`HIRO`, `ATLANT`, ...), and that is all the game has: the executable's only mention of a world is the path it loads the pack from, so the disc supplies no player-facing world name at all.
