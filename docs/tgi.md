# The .TGI files: the artwork format

Each world keeps its artwork in one `.TGI`, between 628 KB and 664 KB. The container is decoded, read off the game's own parser rather than guessed at, and `tools/kula_tgi.py` reads it; which texture the game paints on which face is the level loader's business, and `tools/kula_skins.py` reads that off the executable.

```bash
python3 tools/kula_tgi.py --world HIRO --sections
python3 tools/kula_tgi.py --world HIRO --vram        # the whole VRAM page
python3 tools/kula_tgi.py --world HIRO --textures    # the 56 textures
python3 tools/kula_skins.py                          # what goes on which face, with the addresses
python3 tools/kula_tex.py                            # the atlases the viewer draws with
```

## How it was found

The filenames are built at `0x8004ccf8`, which takes a world index, an extension index into a table of five at `0x80073504` (`.TGI` is the first), and a destination address. `0x8004034c` calls it with extension 0 and destination `0x8013a000`, and `0x800403a0` then hands that address to `0x800253e0`, which is the parser.

## Settled

**A 400-byte header, then eleven sections.** The parser reads eleven `u32` at header offsets 356 to 396, each a count of `u16`, and walks cumulative pointers from `base + 400`. The eleventh boundary lands exactly on the file size in all ten worlds, which is what says the reading is right.

**The last section is not a picture, it is a list of VRAM uploads.** The loop at `0x800254e0` reads `u16 x, y, w, h`, hands the four of them and the bytes that follow to the Psy-Q `LoadImage` at `0x8005c580`, then skips `w * h * 2` bytes and goes round again. So `w` counts 16-bit VRAM words, not pixels, and an 8bpp texture is twice that many pixels wide. The walk consumes every section-10 byte exactly, with nothing left over, in every world.

**Each world ships 56 textures.** They arrive as 32x64-word uploads, which is 64x64 pixels at 8bpp, and each carries three smaller levels: 8x32, 4x16 and 2x8 words. Those only make sense as 32x32, 16x16 and 8x8 at 4bpp, so the mip levels are half-depth. 56 of each, four levels, 224 uploads.

**Palettes are two uploads parked off the side of the page**, 256 words wide at VRAM x=768: one of 72 rows from the top, which is the 72 palettes of 256 colours the textures are paired with, and one from row 90 whose height the world sets, 25 rows in Hiro and 72 in Atlantis, whose rows are the 16-colour palettes the renderer draws through, below. They are ordinary VRAM writes like anything else, which is why looking for them by scanning the file for smooth 16-bit runs found impostors: pixel data read as colour is smooth too. Arctic's stones show that a scan is no way to pick them out either: their palettes hold 23 greys in the dark shade and 25 in the bright, which a scan asking for 32 distinct words refuses, so the atlas reads the rows a texture's record names and nothing else.

**Section 6 pairs each texture with its palettes.** A CLUT id for a palette at VRAM x=768 is `(row << 6) | 48`, so `id & 63 == 48` is a fingerprint, and section 6 is 16.5% such values against a fraction of a percent everywhere else. Its record is 20 `u16`, 427 of them, and the fingerprint falls on fixed slots within that stride.

A record opens with three CLUT ids, and the same three palettes appear again as bare VRAM rows next to the position of each of the texture's four levels: `clut[3]`, then four times over a level's `x, y` and those rows again. Keying on the level-0 position pairs **all 56 textures with exactly one palette triple, in all ten worlds**, and that lack of any contradiction is what says the reading is right rather than merely possible. The 50 records whose position matches no texture point elsewhere in VRAM and are not needed for this.

**The three palettes are three lighting levels.** The header's three fixed-point triples are `0.7 0.6 0.6`, `0.9 0.8 0.8` and `1.2 1.1 1.1`, and the three palettes come out at mean luminance `0.87 : 1.00 : 1.18` in the same order. The measured spread is narrower than the multipliers because 15-bit colour clamps at both ends, but the ordering and the count match, so the game ships each texture pre-shaded three ways rather than lighting it at runtime.

**Section 5 is a table of 119 models, and it is byte-identical in all ten worlds.** Each entry is `i16 first, i16 count` naming a run of section-6 quads (`0x800259b8` reads one by its model's index, four bytes an entry), so a model index means the same thing everywhere and only the pixels behind it change. The copy loop at `0x8002569c` walks it and builds, at `0x800afe94`, a 32-byte entry per quad per shade, laid model by model with the shades of a model side by side; for each quad it works out `u = (x mod 64) * 2` and `v = y mod 256` before calling `getTPage(1, 0, ...)`, whose `tp` of 1 is what says the textures are 8bpp. A model with several quads is an animation or a set of variants, and a face shows the quad its flags' top byte picks: the animator at `0x8004f3b8` writes it once a frame for every face on the lists `0x80026f2c` builds, the laser builder puts a beam's colour there, and the loader puts a 1 there for a face with a pickup on it.

**The models are block faces, and the header at bytes 336 to 352 says how the table is cut**: five groups of 7, 4, 50, 1 and 50. The first 61 models are what a level with a key draws from and the other 58 a level without one, which is every bonus level (the loader at `0x80027b2c` counts the keys on the records of kinds below 5 and sets mode 1 when there are none, and `0x80025c7c` moves the base of the table to model 61 for it). A hidden level would keep the first set whatever its keys, since the same routine marks it mode 2 after the count (`0x80027c74`) and only mode 1 moves the base, though every hidden level has a key. The Simon rooms hold no key either, and draw the first set all the same: the last thing that routine does is read the mode the game is in, at `0x800a33b0`, and put the set back to the first when it is 1 (`0x80027cc4`). Mode 1 is the mode those rooms are played in, which the namer at `0x8004ccf8` says: in it, and only for the level pack, it takes the path one past the ten worlds out of the table at `0x800734d8`, `\HIRO\COPYCAT`, which is the pack the ten Simon rooms are in and the only pack no world's directory holds. The artwork is named without that override, by the world word at `0x800a340c`, and the map, which files the pack under the directory it lives in, draws Hiro's. Each set is the same shape: seven special models, then the world's stones, then one model per object type. In the first set the specials are models 0 to 3, the moving platform's own textures 8, 9, 10 and 11, model 4 the crumbling block's cracked stone at 12, model 5 the four laser plates at 13 to 16 in circuit order, yellow, blue, green, red, and model 6 the invisible block's seven frames at 26 to 32; the stones are models 7 to 10 onto textures 0 to 3, the four the world changes; and models 11 to 60 are indexed by type, so 12 is the fire's five frames at 33 to 37, 13 the ice at 19 and, with a shadow on it, 20, 15 the acid block's green splat at 24, 16, 20 and 21 the round plate at 21 that a teleporter, a switch and a boost button stand on, 18 the exit's red cross and green cross at 17 and 18, 19 the clock face at 25, 22 the moving spikes' four holes at 22, 23 the spikes' plate at 23, and from 37 on, the hidden exit, the arrow, the start and the pickups, the stone plain at 4 and, where a pickup stands, with its painted shadow, texture 5, 6 or 7 by the size of the thing. The second set has one stone, model 68, the animated swirl at 38 to 45; its crumbling block is the grey cracked stone at 54, its plates the green target at 55, and its pickups' models run through the swirl and the swirl with a shadow, 38 to 53. A model whose only quad is texture 8 is a placeholder for a type the loader never puts on a face.

**Which model a face draws is a rule in the loader, at `0x800271d8`**, and `tools/kula_skins.py` reads it and the build ships it under `skins` in `public/objects.json`. For each face of each cell the walk selects on the block's kind where that is not plain and on the type of the object on the face where it is (`0x80027338`; type 29 counts as the clock, `0x8002733c`, and a type from 50, the captivators, as nothing): a plain face draws a stone at random among the world's (`0x800276ac`), a fire, ice or acid block or a face with an object of type `t` draws model 11 + `t` (`0x800276cc`), an invisible block model 6, a crumbling block model 4 and a vanishing block the first stone; the request goes through `0x80028d64`, which builds the face and reads the header word at 300 + 4 × face for its shade. What stands on a face picks the model and nothing more: the walk reads no word of the slot but its type, and lays the face square (`0x8002768c`), save that where the second set is drawn a face of type 20 or more takes a quarter turn from the game's dice (`0x80027710`), as a stone there does. So a clock, which carries 4 in its `f2` on 27 of its 51 placements, and fire and ice are painted the same whatever that field holds, and none of the three is among the types the loader's walk over the objects turns by their `f2`: its table at `0x80010188` sends a clock to the case that skips the turn (`0x80035924`), and fire and ice fall below the table's range to the same case. A moving platform's faces come from the tables at `0x80072f28` (`0x80027cd4`), by axis and by the block's place in the run, each face a model and a turn, or the value the loader takes for a stone at random, which ships beside the tables as `stone`; a laser's two ends wear model 5 on the faces that look along the beam, in the circuit's colour (`0x800280d0`). The header word at 324 says whether a stone may be turned on its face, not at all, a half turn on some or any quarter turn, which only HILLS asks for. The routines at `0x800a3228`, one per face, lay a texture's four corners on the block, and the turn is quarter turns clockwise as seen from outside the face; `kula_skins.py` runs them rather than reading them. The three fixed-point triples at the head of the file are the shades, and the six words at 300 say which face wears which: the top, both x sides and the underside the middle one, the +y side the bright one and the -y side the dark one, except in INCA, COWBOY and HAZE, where the x sides take the bright and the dark.

**A face is built only where no block that hides it stands against it.** Before it builds a face the walk asks the kind of the block beyond (`0x8002dfac`), and each of its four paths has its own list: an ordinary face is not built toward a plain, fire, ice or acid block and is built unseen behind a crumbling one until that goes (`0x800277d8`); the crumbling block's own faces are hidden toward the same five; the invisible block's are not built toward a plain, fire, ice, acid or other invisible block; and the vanishing block's path asks nothing. An empty cell, a beam's cells and every other kind let the face be built, so the stone behind an invisible block, a vanishing one or a platform's home is there to be seen, and through an invisible block it is. The build ships the lists under `skins.hides`, and the kind an ordinary face is built unseen behind apart as `unseen`, since such a face is there to be found though it is not drawn.

**The record table is not a skin table.** A cell holds either a style or a record index, never both, so a block carrying a record has no style of its own, and the record every level ends with holds the level's time. Nothing in a level says how a block looks: the kind and the type say what it is, and the loader's rule above says the rest.

**A face is drawn from the 4bpp levels, through palettes fogged by depth.** The renderer's texture pass at `0x8004f9e0` reads a face's texture entry at 8, 16 or 24 bytes in, the three mip levels, never the 8bpp level at 0, and takes the CLUT from section 0: 72 records of 99 `u16`, one per palette row of the first block, each a CLUT id per step of depth, half a dozen distinct ones down the run, into the second palette block, whose rows hold 16-colour palettes. The 64x64 textures with their 256-colour palettes are what the mips were cut from, and the map draws those.

The table holds block faces and nothing else: a type's model is the face under the thing, not the thing, and the objects themselves are solids in the one `.GGI` on the disc ([ggi.md](ggi.md)).

## Not settled

**Sections 1 to 4 and 7 to 9 are unread.** Section 9 is 225 KB and the largest after the artwork, and the four 2 KB sections at 1 to 4 are suspiciously uniform. The geometry lives somewhere in there.

**Which world's artwork a Simon room is played in.** The namer loads the `.TGI` by the world word, which the menu that enters the Simon mode (`0x80049674`) does not set, and the loader derives from a level counter divided by fifteen (`0x80040288`); what that counter holds in the Simon mode is not traced, so the rooms may wear the world the player came from or always Hiro's.

**What the 8bpp level of each texture is for.** It is uploaded like the rest and the renderer never reads it for a block face; the level select or a near view may.
